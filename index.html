The Nonprofit AI Cookbook · Second Edition

Recipes for getting real work back from AI.

The first edition taught you what to type. This one teaches you what to build — working tools, cleaned-up data, reusable team skills — by doing one thing in the chat window and getting back something that actually runs.

The one idea behind all of it: the hard part was never the perfect prompt. It's giving the AI the context only you have — and every recipe here shows you the shortcut: an artifact you already own (ten Slack threads, three sample rows, the funder's own PDF) instead of a document you'd have to sit down and write. That, plus knowing when to stop fiddling, and climbing from a one-off trick to something your whole team can reuse.

How to read a recipe card

Every recipe is tagged with the same five things. Learn these once and you can size up any recipe in a glance.

🔒
Where data goes
The big one. From “nowhere — it’s yours” to “out to a company.”
💬
What you use
Usually just the chat. Sometimes search, a Project, or a connector you switch on.
🔌
Connections
Does it touch your Drive, inbox, or CRM — or nothing at all?
🧊
Gets smarter?
Set tool (build once) vs. living tool (improves as you feed it).
👤
Who sees it
Just you, your team, or — carefully — the people you serve.

Plus, every card tells you the information you give it — always something you already have (a file, a few sample rows, real examples), never a description you have to sit down and write. If a recipe ever asks you to author a three-page brief first, we got it wrong.
Which AI does this work with? Almost every recipe works in ChatGPT, Gemini, or any modern assistant that can take file uploads and search the web. Where a card says Project, look for ChatGPT's Projects (or a custom GPT) or Gemini's Gems — same idea, different name. The two capstone recipes need an assistant with Gmail/Drive connectors; the rest need only the chat window. A few recipes are marked Companion: they use one named outside tool alongside the chat (like NotebookLM). Our bar for those is strict — one upload or one click, free or already owned, and it works the same way every time.
Don't want to read the steps? Every recipe also comes in a guided version: you paste one prompt, and the AI interviews you and does the work with you. Same recipes, different shape. Browse the guided recipes →

The recipes — a climb, not a list

They're ordered on purpose: easy → hard, personal → organizational, instant payoff → lasting capacity. Start at the top to feel a quick win; keep climbing and your whole org runs the skill.

2
Go get the facts

Enrich My Data

For every entity on your list — orgs, volunteers, donors' employers — the AI goes out to the public web and attaches what it finds: council districts, employers, budget sizes, matching-gift programs. Your database, filled in.

What you need to know — two rules: made-up data looks exactly like found data, so every cell needs a source link, unknowns stay blank, and you spot-check five rows. And check what your data already knows before you go looking — an email domain often answers the question for free.

3
You have more than you think

Remix Your Content

You're sitting on more than you think. Turn one thing you already made — an event description, an annual report, a program flyer — into every format and channel that needs it.

What you need to know: your voice transfers through examples, not adjectives. Show it three past posts you liked instead of describing your tone — then cap it at one pass. If the draft sounds generic, the fix is a better example, not a longer description. Pick the output by where it lives: a file people edit (deck, doc) → ask for the real file; used in a browser (quote cards, a quiz) → a clickable page; a platform (podcast feeds, an LMS) → the AI writes the content, a companion tool performs it.
A word on video: the script, the captions, and the shot list are the AI's job — the footage isn't yet. AI-generated video is expensive, off-brand more often than not, and lands in front of the people you serve. We'll add it when it clears our bar: clear, easy, reliable.

Knowledge on tap

Build an Expert You Can Ask

Load a body of knowledge into a Project — the 400-page manual you dread, your own handbook, a funder's published rubric, a departing colleague's real threads — then just ask it questions all year.

What you need to know: you never write a description of how anyone thinks. You point at an artifact that already exists. And the expert must cite the page every time — never act on an answer it can't quote.

The capacity payoff

Stand Up a Reusable Skill

Turn a workflow you repeat into a shareable skill, so your whole team does it the same way every time. This is where a personal trick becomes an organizational capability.

What you need to know: you teach it with real artifacts — your five best pieces, your best note-taker's actual follow-up email — and it becomes a living tool that sharpens every time you feed it a new example. One “never sound like this” counter-example teaches more than three more good ones.
