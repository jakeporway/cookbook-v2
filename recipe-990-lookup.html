Size Up Any Nonprofit in 30 Seconds

Paste a list of org names and get back each one's budget size, staff count, and program focus from their public 990 filings. It's a fast way to narrow a list of 60 possible partners down to the 15 worth calling first.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

Just the org names, from a list you already have: a coalition roster, a conference attendee list, a funder's grantee page. If you have EINs, include them (an EIN is the org's federal tax ID, like a Social Security number for nonprofits). If you don't, names work fine. Everything the AI looks up from there is public record.

The recipe
  1. Gather your list of org names
    Names alone work. Adding a city or state helps when a name is common, since there's a "Community Action" in almost every county. EINs remove any ambiguity if you have them.
  2. Paste the list with this prompt
    Point the AI at the structured public dataset instead of letting it search the web loosely. That's what keeps the results consistent.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Here is a list of nonprofits I want a quick read on:

[paste org names or EINs, one per line — add city/state
if the name is common]

For each org, look up its most recent public Form 990.
Use ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer
(projects.propublica.org/nonprofits) as your first stop —
it has every filing, free.

Return one table: Org | EIN | Most recent 990 year |
Total revenue | Total expenses | Employees | One-line
program focus (from the mission statement) | Source link.

For every row, the source link must go to that org's
ProPublica page or the 990 itself. If you can't find an
org or a specific figure, leave the cell blank and write
UNKNOWN — do not guess or estimate. Flag any name that
matches more than one nonprofit so I can pick the right one.
    ROLE:
You are a nonprofit research assistant who works only from primary
sources — the filings themselves, not summaries or third-party
write-ups of them.

GOAL:
Give me a one-screen triage read on the organizations listed below:
how big each one is, how many people it employs, and what it
actually does — with every figure traceable back to the filing it
came from.

CONTEXT:
The organizations:

[paste org names or EINs, one per line — add city/state
if the name is common]

Form 990 is the annual return every US nonprofit files publicly.
ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer
(projects.propublica.org/nonprofits) has every
filing, free — start there. Note that 990s run one to two years
behind, so "most recent" may describe a two-year-old picture.

STEPS:
1. For each organization, find its most recent public Form 990.
2. Record: EIN, filing year, total revenue, total expenses,
   number of employees, and a one-line program focus drawn from
   the mission statement in the filing.
3. Capture a source link per row that points to that org's
   ProPublica page or the 990 itself.
4. Where a name matches more than one nonprofit, do not pick one.
   Flag it and list the candidates so I can choose.
5. Where you cannot find an org or a specific figure, leave the
   cell blank and write UNKNOWN. Do not guess, estimate, or
   interpolate from a different year.

OUTPUT:
One table: Org | EIN | Most recent 990 year | Total revenue |
Total expenses | Employees | One-line program focus | Source link.

Below the table, two short lists: names that matched more than one
nonprofit, and rows where anything came back UNKNOWN.
  3. Resolve the flagged name collisions
    Where two orgs share a name, click both ProPublica links and pick yours by city. Tell the AI which one is right and it will correct the row.
  4. Spot-check 5 random rows against the linked filings
    Open the source link and confirm the revenue and year match. Note the filing year while you're there. 990s run one to two years behind, so "most recent" may describe 2024 rather than today.
  5. Sort and shortlist
    Ask the AI to sort the table by revenue, or to group the orgs into rough size bands. Now you have a short list of who to call first, with a source link behind every number.
990s run one to two years behind. The "current" budget you quote in a meeting may be two years old. Say the filing year out loud when you cite it.
What you walk away with

A one-screen table with budget size, staff count, and program focus for every org on your list, each row linked to the filing it came from. Work that used to mean opening sixty PDFs now takes about half a minute per org.

Made-up data looks exactly like found data. An invented revenue figure reads just as plausibly as a real one. So: a source link in every row, unknowns left blank, and a spot-check of 5 random rows against the filings before you act on any of it.
When to stop using this recipe. For dozens of orgs, this recipe is the right tool. For hundreds, or a refresh every month, ask the AI to build a small tool that calls ProPublica's free Nonprofit Explorer API directly. For thousands, or anything business-critical, you need a real data service.
