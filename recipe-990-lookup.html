Size Up Any Nonprofit in 30 Seconds

Paste a list of org names; get back each one's budget size, staff count, and program focus from their public 990 filings — the triage that turns "60 potential partners" into "the 15 worth calling first."

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

Just the org names (or EINs, if you have them — an EIN is the org's federal tax ID, like a Social Security number for nonprofits; don't have them? Names work fine) from whatever list you're already staring at — a coalition roster, a conference attendee list, a funder's grantee page. Everything from there is public record.

The recipe
  1. Gather your list of org names
    Names alone work; adding a city or state helps when a name is common ("Community Action" exists in every county in America). EINs, if you have them, remove all ambiguity.
  2. Paste the list with this prompt
    The key move: point the AI at the structured public dataset instead of letting it search loosely.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Here is a list of nonprofits I want a quick read on:

[paste org names or EINs, one per line — add city/state
if the name is common]

For each org, look up its most recent public Form 990.
Use ProPublica's Nonprofit Explorer
(projects.propublica.org/nonprofits) as your first stop —
it has every filing, free.

Return one table: Org | EIN | Most recent 990 year |
Total revenue | Total expenses | Employees | One-line
program focus (from the mission statement) | Source link.

For every row, the source link must go to that org's
ProPublica page or the 990 itself. If you can't find an
org or a specific figure, leave the cell blank and write
UNKNOWN — do not guess or estimate. Flag any name that
matches more than one nonprofit so I can pick the right one.
  3. Resolve the flagged name collisions
    Where two orgs share a name, click both ProPublica links, pick yours by city, and tell the AI which one — it'll correct the row.
  4. Spot-check 5 random rows against the linked filings
    Open the source link and confirm revenue and year match. Also note the filing year — 990s run one to two years behind, so "most recent" may mean 2024's picture, not today's.
  5. Sort and shortlist
    Ask the AI to sort the table by revenue, or to group orgs into rough size bands. Sixty names just became the fifteen worth calling first — with receipts.
990s run one to two years behind. The "current" budget you quote in a meeting may be two years old — say the filing year out loud when you cite it.
What you walk away with

A one-screen triage table — budget size, staff count, and program focus for every org on your list, each row linked to the actual filing. The 30-second size-up that used to mean opening sixty PDFs.

Made-up data looks exactly like found data. An invented revenue figure looks just as plausible as a real one. The rule: a source link in every row, unknowns stay blank, and spot-check 5 random rows against the filings before you act on any of it.
The lookup line. Dozens of orgs → this recipe. Hundreds, or a refresh every month → ask the AI to build a tool that calls ProPublica's free Nonprofit Explorer API directly. Thousands, or business-critical → you've outgrown the recipe; that's a data service.
