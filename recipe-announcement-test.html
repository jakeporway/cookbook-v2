Test how an announcement will land before you send it

Before the difficult announcement goes out — a program closing, a fee increase, a leadership change — it reads your draft as each audience: the worried parent, the longtime donor, the skeptical board member. You hear the objections while you can still edit.

Information you give it

Your draft, plus one honest paragraph of context you'd never publish — what's really driving the decision, what you're worried about — and a list of your real audiences.

The recipe
  1. Write the draft the normal way first
    Draft the announcement as you normally would, before this recipe touches it.
  2. Define audiences specifically
    "A parent whose child is in the closing program," not "stakeholders." Specific audiences produce specific, useful reactions.
  3. Paste this prompt
    Fill in your real audiences and paste your draft along with the honest context paragraph.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Chat prompt
    Read this draft announcement as each of the following people,
one at a time: [YOUR AUDIENCES — e.g. a parent whose child is in
the closing program; a longtime monthly donor; a skeptical board
member]

For each person, tell me:
1. Their first emotional reaction, sentence by sentence where
   relevant.
2. The questions they'd immediately have that the draft doesn't
   answer.
3. Anything they could reasonably misread, and the worst-faith
   reading a critic could quote.
4. What this person most needs to hear that's missing.

Do not soften your readings to spare me — a gentle reading now
costs me a hard Monday later. Do NOT rewrite the announcement.

Context you should know but that never gets published:
[YOUR HONEST PARAGRAPH]
  4. Read the reactions and revise yourself
    The judgment calls — what to concede, what to hold — are leadership, not editing. Make them yourself.
  5. Re-run once on the revision, then stop
    Two passes is rehearsal; more is stalling. Send the final version through your real approval chain.
What you walk away with

A list of the real objections and gaps your announcement will meet, from each audience that matters, while you can still edit — plus a revised draft that answers what it can before it goes out.

It predicts reactions plausibly, not perfectly — treat the output as rehearsal, not research. If the stakes are high enough, this recipe complements — never replaces — showing the draft to one real, trusted member of the affected audience.
