Your annual report is 40 social posts

The report you sweated over contains months of content: stat callouts, story excerpts, milestone posts, quote-graphic copy. Mine it once, schedule it all quarter. You're sitting on more than you think.

Information you give it

The finished annual report PDF, plus 3 past posts that actually performed well. The report supplies the substance; the three posts supply your voice — real artifacts, not a brand guide or a description of your tone.

The recipe
  1. Grab the report PDF and your 3 best posts
    Open your analytics (or just your memory) and copy the three posts from the past year that got real engagement. Those three are your voice sample — choose ones you'd happily post again.
  2. Open a normal chat and attach the report
    Use the paperclip to attach the PDF. No special mode needed.
  3. Paste this prompt with your posts dropped in
    The page-number rule matters: it turns every claim into something you can check in ten seconds.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Attached is our finished annual report. Here are three of our past
social posts that performed well — match their voice and length,
not a generic nonprofit voice:

POST 1: [paste it]
POST 2: [paste it]
POST 3: [paste it]

Mine the report for social content. Give me 40 posts, organized
in four groups of ten:
1. STAT CALLOUTS — one striking number each, with a line of context
2. STORY EXCERPTS — short human moments pulled from the narratives
3. MILESTONES — anniversaries, firsts, and "this year we…" moments
4. QUOTE GRAPHICS — a pull-quote plus a one-line caption to post it with

Rules: use only what's actually in the report — invent nothing.
Label every post with the report page it came from so I can
double-check it. Write for [Instagram and LinkedIn].

Give me your best draft of each. I'll pick and lightly edit —
don't offer endless variations. If you can't find 40 clean,
well-sourced pieces, give me fewer. Don't stretch.
  4. Spot-check the numbers against the pages
    Every post carries a page label — check each stat and quote against that page before it goes anywhere near a scheduler. Checking 40 posts against their pages takes as long as generating them — that hour is the job, not overhead.
  5. Pick, lightly edit, and cut without guilt
    If 28 of the 40 are keepers, that's still a full quarter of content. One editing pass, then stop.
  6. Load your scheduler for the quarter
    Paste the keepers into your scheduling tool, two or three a week. A human schedules and sends everything — the queue is yours, not the chat's.
What you walk away with

A quarter's social calendar mined from work you already did — 30–40 page-cited posts in four content types, ready for your scheduler. And the remix-matrix habit: one strong source document is never just one thing.

If the posts sound generic, the fix is a better example — not a longer description. Swap in a past post that sounds more like you and run it once more. Cap iteration at one pass: the "make it punchier… warmer… shorter?" spiral wastes the afternoon this recipe just gave back. Draft, spot-check, pick, schedule.
