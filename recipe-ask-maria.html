Information you give it

10–15 real email and Slack threads where Maria is actually solving problems — threads she chooses and she redacts — plus her handoff doc. Nobody writes a "how Maria thinks" essay. Ten real conversations carry her judgment better than any description of it, and she'd never write the essay anyway.

The recipe
  1. Ask Maria — and mean it as a question
    Explain what you want to build and why, and that she controls every thread that goes in. If she says no, this recipe ends here. If she says yes, block half a day on her calendar before her last week — this is real handoff work, not a favor.
  2. Maria picks 10–15 threads where she's solving problems
    Not announcements — judgment in action: the site-visit scheduling mess, the vendor who needed the firm-but-warm email, the funder report that almost went out wrong. She copies each into a doc and redacts as she goes: client names, personal asides, anything a colleague wouldn't want preserved. Her threads mention other people — clients, colleagues, partners. Before anything is uploaded, have whoever handles HR or compliance skim the selected threads. Non-negotiable if you work in health, childcare, or social services.
  3. Create the Project and upload her picks
    In your AI chat: Projects → New project → "Ask Maria." Upload the redacted threads (one file per thread, named for the problem: "site-visit-scheduling.pdf") and her handoff doc via Add content. Maria gets final look at the file list — her yes covers exactly what's in it.
  4. Paste these Project instructions
    Open the Project's Instructions box and paste this. The refusal line matters as much as the voice line.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Project instructions
    You are "Ask Maria" — a record of how [Maria], our longtime
[program coordinator], handled things during her [12] years here.
Your ONLY knowledge is the threads and handoff doc in this
Project, which she chose and redacted herself.

When asked how something was handled, answer in Maria's voice and
approach, grounded ONLY in what her threads actually show. Always
say which thread you're drawing from and quote the key lines, so
the reader can go look at the real exchange.

If the threads don't cover a question, say exactly that: "Maria's
threads don't cover this." Never guess what she would have done —
a wrong answer in her voice is worse than no answer.

You describe how things WERE handled; you don't decide how they
should be handled now. For current decisions, end with: "That's
how Maria approached it — the call on today's situation is
yours."
  5. Test it together before she leaves
    Maria and her successor ask it five real questions side by side. Maria grades the answers — "yes, that's what I'd say" or "no, that thread was a special case." Gaps she spots become one or two more threads while she can still add them.
  6. Hand it to the successor — as a starting point, not an oracle
    The successor's first months change: instead of archaeology through a stranger's inbox, they ask "how did we handle the site-visit scheduling mess?" and get the actual thread. What they decide to do next is theirs.
What you walk away with

Twelve years of tacit knowledge that doesn't walk out the door: an askable record of how Maria actually handled the hard stuff, in her words, with every answer traceable to a real thread. And Maria leaves knowing exactly what was kept — because she chose it. Decide now when this Project gets archived or deleted — a year after the successor is settled is a fine default. Write it down.

Consent is a step, not a footnote. Maria picks the threads. Maria redacts them. Maria says yes — to the specific file list, not the general idea. Never build this from someone's messages without them: it isn't just an ethics problem, it's a quality problem too, because only Maria knows which threads show her real judgment and which were exceptions. And other people are in those threads — her redaction pass protects them as much as her.
