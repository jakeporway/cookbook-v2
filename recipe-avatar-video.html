Make a training video without filming anyone

Record yourself once — a short video and voice sample — then type any script and your avatar presents it on camera. When the policy changes, you edit the script instead of re-booking a filming afternoon.

Data leaves to a third party
Your likeness is stored by the vendor. It's locked to your own account, and every video this makes carries an invisible SynthID watermark that marks it as AI-made. Still — a recording of your face and voice, kept by a company, is a real step up in what's at stake.
Public-facing output
Viewers should be told it's an AI presenter. A human approves every script before it becomes a video, and the disclosure — spoken and written — travels with it every time.
Information you give it

One selfie-style recording and a voice sample — made once, inside Vids — then a script per video. Draft each script in your AI chat from your existing training document; the chat never sees your face or voice.

The recipe
  1. Check your access first
    Personal avatars in Google Vids are available on Google AI Pro/Ultra and on Workspace business tiers, and are region- and 18+-gated. If your org is on Google Workspace for Nonprofits, check your current Gemini/Vids access in the admin console — entitlements have been changing during 2026, so verify what your plan includes today rather than trusting a blog post.
  2. Create your avatar in Vids — your own likeness only
    The tool enforces this, and so should your policy: no avatars of colleagues, ever, even with permission in writing this year. Follow the recording prompts once; that recording is reused for every future video.
  3. Draft the script in your AI chat
    Turn your existing training document into something meant to be heard, not read.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] part; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Turn this training document into a 3-minute spoken script. Short
sentences, one idea per sentence, written to be heard, not read.
Plain words. Add a spoken note at the start that this presenter is
a digital avatar of [name].
  4. Paste the script into Vids, generate, and watch every second
    Don't skim it. Watch the whole thing before it goes anywhere, the same as you'd review a real recording.
  5. Disclose it, every time
    The video says it's an avatar, and the description says so too. This isn't optional polish — it's the line that keeps this recipe honest.
What you walk away with

A presenter you never have to re-book. Policy changes become a script edit and a five-minute regeneration, not a filming afternoon.

This is the one recipe in the book where the tool imitates a person. The lines that keep it clean: your likeness only, disclosure always, a human approves every script, and nothing constituent-facing goes out unwatched. If any of those feel heavy for your org, that's a sign to keep using it for internal training only.
