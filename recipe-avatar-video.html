Make a training video without filming anyone

Record yourself once, a short video and a voice sample, then type any script and your avatar presents it on camera. When the policy changes, you edit the script instead of booking another filming afternoon.

Data leaves to a third party
Your likeness is stored by the vendor. It's locked to your own account, and every video it makes carries an invisible SynthID watermark identifying it as AI-made. Even so, a recording of your face and voice held by a company raises the stakes considerably.
Public-facing output
Viewers should be told it's an AI presenter. A person approves every script before it becomes a video, and the disclosure travels with it every time, both spoken and written.
Information you give it

One selfie-style recording and a voice sample, both made once inside Vids, and then a script for each video. Draft the scripts in your AI chat from your existing training document. The chat never sees your face or voice.

The recipe
  1. Check your access first
    Personal avatars in Google Vids are available on Google AI Pro/Ultra and on Workspace business tiers, and they're restricted by region and to users 18 and over. If your org is on Google Workspace for Nonprofits, check your current Gemini/Vids access in the admin console. Entitlements have changed several times during 2026, so verify what your plan includes today rather than trusting a blog post.
  2. Create your avatar in Vids, using your own likeness only
    The tool enforces this and your policy should too: no avatars of colleagues, even with written permission. Follow the recording prompts once. That recording is reused for every future video.
  3. Draft the script in your AI chat
    Turn your existing training document into a script meant to be heard rather than read.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] part and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Turn this training document into a 3-minute spoken script. Short
sentences, one idea per sentence, written to be heard, not read.
Plain words. Add a spoken note at the start that this presenter is
a digital avatar of [name].
    ROLE:
You are a scriptwriter for spoken video. You write for the ear,
not the page.

GOAL:
Turn the attached training document into a script a presenter can
read aloud in about three minutes.

CONTEXT:
The document is attached. It will be delivered by a digital
avatar of [name], not by a person on camera — so the script has
to disclose that up front.

STEPS:
1. Read the document and keep only what a viewer needs to hear.
2. Write short sentences, one idea per sentence, in plain words.
   Read each line back to yourself — if it needs a second pass to
   follow, rewrite it.
3. Open with a spoken note that this presenter is a digital
   avatar of [name].
4. Keep every instruction, number, and safety point exactly as
   the document states it.

OUTPUT:
A clean spoken script of about three minutes, disclosure line
first, no stage directions or slide notes unless I ask.
  4. Paste the script into Vids, generate, and watch every second
    Don't skim it. Watch the whole thing before it goes anywhere, the same as you'd review a real recording.
  5. Disclose it every time
    The video itself says it's an avatar, and the description says so too. Treat the disclosure as required rather than as optional polish.
What you walk away with

A presenter you never have to re-book. A policy change becomes a script edit and a five-minute regeneration instead of a filming afternoon.

This is the one recipe in the book where the tool imitates a person. Four rules keep it in bounds: your likeness only, disclosure every time, a person approves every script, and nothing constituent-facing goes out unwatched. If any of those feel like too much for your org, use this for internal training only.
