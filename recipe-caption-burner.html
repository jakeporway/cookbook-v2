Remix · Video

Add captions to any video

A small tool that runs on your own computer: drop a video on it, get the video back with captions burned in — for accessibility, social, and everyone watching with the sound off. The video never uploads anywhere.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

Nothing sensitive — you describe what you want the tool to do. No video is attached at build time; your first real video only ever touches the finished tool, in your own browser.

The recipe
  1. Open a normal chat and describe the tool
    No video needed yet — you're describing what the finished tool should do, not showing it anything.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Copy it as-is.

    Prompt
    Build me a single self-contained HTML file: I drop a video file on
it, it transcribes the speech on my own machine using a browser
speech-recognition model (such as Whisper via transformers.js),
shows me the captions as editable text with timestamps, lets me
fix any word, then burns the corrected captions into the video
(ffmpeg compiled for the browser is acceptable) and lets me
download the result. Everything must run locally in the browser —
the video must never upload to any server. Show a clear progress
bar, because this takes time.
  2. Save the file, then test with a short clip first
    Something under a minute. The first run downloads a speech-recognition model — hundreds of megabytes, one time only — so give it a minute before you decide something's broken.
  3. Edit the captions before you burn them in
    Names and program terms are exactly what it gets wrong. The editing pane is the human-in-the-loop step — use it every time, not just when something looks off.
  4. Expect it to be slow — that's the trade for local
    A ten-minute video can take a while on an older laptop, especially the first time. That slowness is the price of the video never leaving your machine.
What you walk away with

A downloaded video with captions burned in, and a reusable tool for the next one — with no per-minute fee and no upload, ever.

Local isn't always the right call, and that's fine. If a video is public anyway and speed matters more than privacy, uploading it to a captioning service is a legitimate choice. This recipe exists for the videos you'd never upload — and for never paying per-minute again.
