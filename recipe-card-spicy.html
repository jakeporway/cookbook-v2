The outreach drafter

Connect your CRM so the AI can pull real participant records and draft personalized outreach messages — a powerful workflow that also carries real privacy and accuracy risk you must manage.

Where data goes
Out to a
company
Real records leave your system. Participant data is sent to an outside company, passes through the AI, and is stored on their servers — where it could in principle be reached by a subpoena or used by the vendor under their terms.
Riskiest of 4 levels · check your data policy first

Front-facing (constituent). The output is written to go to the people you serve — so a human must read and approve every message before it's sent. Never let it send on its own.
3 levels: Individual → Team → Front-facing. This is the one to handle with care.

The recipe
  1. Confirm you're allowed to do this
    Before anything else: check that your org's data policy permits sending participant records to an outside AI service. If you're unsure, stop and ask. This is the real gate.
  2. Connect your CRM in a Project
    An admin links your CRM (and optionally your shared drive) so the AI can read contact records and your message templates.
  3. Give it your voice and your rules
    Paste a few of your best past messages and your hard rules (tone, what never to say, how to address people). This is what makes it a living tool that improves over time.
    Prompt
    You draft outreach to our participants. Pull the contact's
history from the CRM, then write a short, warm message in
our voice. Here are examples of messages that worked:
[paste 3]. Hard rules: [never promise funding, always
use their preferred name, keep under 120 words].
Draft only — I will review and send every message myself.
  4. Review every draft by hand, then send
    The AI drafts; you are the editor and the sender. Read each message for accuracy and tone before it ever reaches a person. The AI never sends on its own.
What you walk away with

A reusable drafting partner that pulls real context from your CRM and writes personalized outreach in your voice — cutting hours of writing to minutes, as long as a human stays in the loop on every send.
