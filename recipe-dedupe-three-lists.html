Your Data, Handled · Dedupe

Find the Same People Across Your Three Lists

Your event platform, your mailing list, and your donor CRM each hold their own version of everybody. This builds a tool that runs on your own computer and gives you one deduplicated list, plus a separate pile of possible matches for you to decide on.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

Just the column headers of each export: the single top row from your Eventbrite, Mailchimp, and donor-CRM CSVs. Not the rows underneath, and not a single name or email. The tool does all the actual matching on your own machine.

The recipe
  1. Export each list as a CSV and set the files aside
    Eventbrite, Mailchimp, and your donor CRM each have an export button. Save all three files somewhere you can find them. None of this gets pasted into the AI.
  2. Copy the top row of each file, then paste this prompt
    Open each CSV (or the platform's column list) and grab just the header line. Swap your three sets of headers into the highlighted parts.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    I have three CSV exports of people, and many people appear on more
than one list. I will NOT paste the data: here are just the column
headers of each file:

List 1: Eventbrite export: [First Name, Last Name, Email, Order Date]
List 2: Mailchimp export: [Email Address, First Name, Last Name, Tags]
List 3: Little Green Light export: [Name, Email, Phone, Last Gift Date]

Build me a SINGLE self-contained HTML file (no internet needed, no
libraries loaded from the web) where I can drop all three CSVs onto
the page. It must read the files in my browser only, my data should
never be uploaded anywhere, and show me:

1. One merged, deduplicated list. Match people by email address first.
   Where emails differ or are missing, compare names, catch nicknames
   ("Bob"/"Robert"), small typos, and reversed first/last names.
2. A separate "These might be the same person, you decide" table for
   every uncertain match, showing the rows side by side with a
   "Same person" / "Different people" button on each pair. NEVER
   auto-merge an uncertain match.
3. On each merged row, a note showing which of the three lists that
   person came from.
4. A button to download the final combined list as a CSV.
    ROLE:
You are building me a small, self-contained tool. I am not a
coder, assume I will open one file and use it.

GOAL:
Merge three exported people-lists into one deduplicated list,
without ever guessing that two people are the same person.

CONTEXT:
I have three CSV exports of people, and many people appear on more
than one list. I will NOT paste the data: here are just the
column headers of each file:

List 1: Eventbrite export: [First Name, Last Name, Email, Order Date]
List 2: Mailchimp export: [Email Address, First Name, Last Name, Tags]
List 3: Little Green Light export: [Name, Email, Phone, Last Gift Date]

STEPS:
Build a SINGLE self-contained HTML file (no internet needed, no
libraries loaded from the web) where I drop all three CSVs onto
the page. It must read the files in my browser only, my data
should never be uploaded anywhere. It should:
1. Match people by email address first. Where emails differ or
   are missing, compare names, catching nicknames ("Bob" /
   "Robert"), small typos, and reversed first/last names.
2. NEVER auto-merge an uncertain match. Every uncertain pair goes
   to a separate review table instead.
3. Note on each merged row which of the three lists that person
   came from.

OUTPUT:
In the page:
1. One merged, deduplicated list.
2. A "These might be the same person, you decide" table showing
   each uncertain pair side by side, with "Same person" /
   "Different people" buttons.
3. The source-list note on every merged row.
4. A button to download the final combined list as a CSV.
  3. Download the HTML file the AI hands back
    It arrives as a file you can click. Save it next to your exports.
  4. Drop all three real CSVs onto the page
    The merged list and the "you decide" pile appear right away, and none of those names ever left your computer.
  5. Work through the "you decide" pile yourself
    This is the step that matters. The tool shows you each uncertain pair side by side and you click Same or Different. Five minutes of your judgment is worth more than a bad automatic merge that mails one donor twice or thanks the wrong Roberta.
  6. Download the clean list and reuse the tool before every mailing
    Fresh exports in, clean list out. No rebuilding, no re-prompting.
What you walk away with

One clean, deduplicated mailing list you can download as a CSV, plus a reusable tool you'll open before every mailing. Because the uncertain matches land in a pile that you resolve, nobody gets merged out of existence without a person saying so.

The fuzzy match is a smart guess rather than a certainty. "J. Martinez" and "Jose Martinez" at different emails might be one person or two. It will catch the obvious cases, like Bob and Robert or a typo'd email, but it won't catch everything. That's what the "you decide" pile is for: it puts a person between a questionable merge and your next mailing. Never skip it, and never ask the tool to merge everything automatically.
