Find the Same People Across Your Three Lists

Your event platform, your mailing list, and your donor CRM each have their own version of everybody. Get one deduplicated list — plus a "these might be the same person, you decide" pile — from a tool that runs on your own computer.

Information you give it

Just the column headers of each export — the single top row from your Eventbrite, Mailchimp, and donor-CRM CSVs. Not the rows underneath, not a single name or email. The tool does all the actual matching on your machine.

The recipe
  1. Export each list as a CSV — and set the files aside
    Eventbrite, Mailchimp, your donor CRM: each has an export button. Save all three files somewhere handy. You won't paste any of this into the AI.
  2. Copy the top row of each file, then paste this prompt
    Open each CSV (or the platform's column list) and grab just the header line. Swap your three sets of headers into the highlighted parts.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    I have three CSV exports of people, and many people appear on more
than one list. I will NOT paste the data — here are just the column
headers of each file:

List 1 — Eventbrite export: [First Name, Last Name, Email, Order Date]
List 2 — Mailchimp export: [Email Address, First Name, Last Name, Tags]
List 3 — Little Green Light export: [Name, Email, Phone, Last Gift Date]

Build me a SINGLE self-contained HTML file (no internet needed, no
libraries loaded from the web) where I can drop all three CSVs onto
the page. It must read the files in my browser only — my data should
never be uploaded anywhere — and show me:

1. One merged, deduplicated list. Match people by email address first.
   Where emails differ or are missing, compare names — catch nicknames
   ("Bob"/"Robert"), small typos, and reversed first/last names.
2. A separate "These might be the same person — you decide" table for
   every uncertain match, showing the rows side by side with a
   "Same person" / "Different people" button on each pair. NEVER
   auto-merge an uncertain match.
3. On each merged row, a note showing which of the three lists that
   person came from.
4. A button to download the final combined list as a CSV.
  3. Download the HTML file the AI hands back
    It arrives as a file you can click. Save it next to your exports.
  4. Drop all three real CSVs onto the page
    The merged list and the "you decide" pile appear instantly — and none of those names ever left your computer.
  5. Work through the "you decide" pile yourself
    This is the step that matters. The tool shows you each uncertain pair side by side; you click Same or Different. Five minutes of your judgment beats a bad auto-merge that mails one donor twice — or thanks the wrong Roberta.
  6. Download the clean list and reuse the tool before every mailing
    Fresh exports in, clean list out. No rebuilding, no re-prompting.
What you walk away with

One clean, deduplicated mailing list you can download as a CSV — plus a reusable tool you'll open before every mailing. And because the uncertain matches land in a pile that you resolve, no one gets merged out of existence without a human saying so.

The fuzzy match is a smart guess, not gospel. "J. Martinez" and "Jose Martinez" at different emails might be one person or two. It will catch the obvious ones — Bob/Robert, a typo'd email — but it won't catch everything. That's exactly what the "you decide" pile is for: the human-in-the-loop that catches bad merges before they reach a mail-merge. Never skip it, and never ask the tool to auto-merge everything.
