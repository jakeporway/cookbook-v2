Which District Is Everyone In?

Paste your list of partner orgs or program sites with their addresses and get back each one's city council district, the councilmember's name and contact details, and a source link on every row. You end up knowing which elected official to put each org in front of.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

Your existing list of partner orgs or program sites with their street addresses, which is the spreadsheet you already keep. Org addresses are public record, so this is one of the few cases where you can paste freely. Client home addresses are not public. See the caution below.

The recipe
  1. Check whose addresses these are
    Partner orgs, program sites, and coalition members are public, so you can paste them. If the list contains anyone's home address, stop and use the local-tool version in step 6 instead.
  2. Open a normal chat and paste your list with this prompt
    Two columns is plenty: name and address. Swap in your city.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Here is a list of partner organizations in [New York City] with
their public street addresses:

[paste your rows: Org name, Address]

For each row, search the web to find:
1. Which city council district that address falls in.
2. The current councilmember for that district, name, plus a
   link to their official contact page.

Return one table: Org | Address | District | Councilmember |
Contact link | Source.

For every row, include a source link for what you found (the
official district-lookup or council page you used). If you can't
find or confirm something, leave the cell blank and write
UNKNOWN, do not guess. At the end, tell me which rows you are
least confident about.
    ROLE:
You are a civic research assistant. You work from official
district-lookup and council sources, and you show your work.

GOAL:
Tell me which political district each of our partner
organizations sits in, and who represents it, with a link I can
check for every row.

CONTEXT:
Here is a list of partner organizations in [New York City] with their
public street addresses:

[paste your rows: Org name, Address]

These are business addresses, already public. Boundaries change
with redistricting, so use current official sources rather than
memory.

STEPS:
1. For each row, search the web to find which city council district that
   address falls in.
2. Find the current representative for that district, name, plus
   a link to their official contact page.
3. Record a source link for what you found: the official
   district-lookup tool or council page you actually used.
4. If you cannot find or confirm something, leave the cell blank
   and write UNKNOWN. Do not guess, and do not infer a district
   from a nearby address.

OUTPUT:
One table: Org | Address | District | Councilmember | Contact
link | Source.

After the table, tell me which rows you are least confident about
and why.
  3. Scan the UNKNOWNs and low-confidence rows first
    Ambiguous addresses land here: suite numbers, new buildings, streets that cross district lines. Fix those rows by hand. There are usually only a few.
  4. Spot-check 5 random rows against the linked sources
    Click the source link and confirm the district matches. A row counts as confirmed when the page you land on names the address, street, or neighborhood, rather than only the district number. Five clean rows means you can trust the rest. One wrong row means you check them all.
  5. Paste the table back into your spreadsheet
    Keep the source-link column. In the middle of budget season you'll want to re-verify a row in one click.
  6. Build the local version for home addresses or hundreds of rows
    Ask the AI to build a single HTML page that looks districts up on your own machine using the free Census geocoder and public district boundaries. Addresses never enter the chat at all.
What you walk away with

Your partner list with a district, councilmember, and contact link attached to every org, and a source link on each row so anyone on the team can verify before they pick up the phone. It's the map you want going into budget season.

Made-up data looks exactly like found data. A hallucinated district number is indistinguishable from a real one. The rule on every lookup recipe is the same: a source link in every cell, unknowns left blank, and a spot-check of 5 random rows before you act on any of it.
When to stop using this recipe. For dozens of rows, chat handles it. For hundreds of rows, or a refresh every month, ask the AI to build a tool that calls the public dataset directly, using the free Census geocoder and district boundary files. For thousands of rows, you need a real data service.
Org addresses are public. Client home addresses are not. Never paste anyone's home address into a chat. For clients, use the local-tool version in step 6, where the lookup runs entirely on your own computer.
