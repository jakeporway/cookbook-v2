Which District Is Everyone In?

Paste your list of partner orgs or program sites with addresses; get back each one's city council district, the councilmember's name and contact, and a source link on every row — so you know exactly which elected to put each org in front of.

Information you give it

Your existing list of partner orgs or program sites with their street addresses — the spreadsheet you already keep. Org addresses are public record, so this is a rare paste-freely case. Client home addresses are not — see the caution below.

The recipe
  1. Check whose addresses these are
    Partner orgs, program sites, coalition members — public, paste away. Anyone's home address — stop; use the local-tool variant in step 6 instead.
  2. Open a normal chat and paste your list with this prompt
    Two columns is plenty: name and address. Swap in your city.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Here is a list of partner organizations in [New York City] with
their public street addresses:

[paste your rows: Org name, Address]

For each row, search the web to find:
1. Which city council district that address falls in.
2. The current councilmember for that district — name, plus a
   link to their official contact page.

Return one table: Org | Address | District | Councilmember |
Contact link | Source.

For every row, include a source link for what you found (the
official district-lookup or council page you used). If you can't
find or confirm something, leave the cell blank and write
UNKNOWN — do not guess. At the end, tell me which rows you are
least confident about.
  3. Scan the UNKNOWNs and low-confidence rows first
    Ambiguous addresses (suite numbers, new buildings, streets that cross district lines) land here. Fix those rows by hand — they're usually only a few.
  4. Spot-check 5 random rows against the linked sources
    Click the source link, confirm the district matches. You've confirmed a row when the page you land on names the address, street, or neighborhood — not just the district number. Five clean rows is your green light; one wrong row means check them all.
  5. Paste the table back into your spreadsheet
    Keep the source-link column. Future-you, mid-budget-season, will want to re-verify in one click.
  6. Home addresses, or hundreds of rows? Build the local variant
    Ask the AI to build a single HTML page that looks districts up on your own machine using the free Census geocoder and public district boundaries. Addresses never enter the chat at all.
What you walk away with

Your partner list with a district, councilmember, and contact link attached to every org — and a source link per row so anyone on the team can verify before they pick up the phone. Exactly the map you need walking into budget season.

Made-up data looks exactly like found data. A hallucinated district number is indistinguishable from a real one. The rule on every enrichment recipe: a source link in every cell, unknowns stay blank, and spot-check 5 random rows before you act on any of it.
The lookup line. Dozens of rows → this recipe handles it in chat. Hundreds of rows, or every month → ask the AI to build a tool that calls the public dataset directly (the free Census geocoder + district boundary files). Thousands → you've outgrown the recipe; that's a data service.
Org addresses are public. Client home addresses are not. Never paste anyone's home address into a chat. For clients, use the local-tool variant (step 6) — the lookup runs entirely on your own computer.
