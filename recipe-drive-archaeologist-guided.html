The Drive Archaeologist

Connect the AI to your shared drive and ask the questions that come up constantly: which of these six "FINAL" budgets is the real one, where the current logo lives, what you promised a funder last year. The access is read-only, every answer cites a file, and you open the file yourself to confirm.

Connects to your accounts
Read-only access to your files. This is a permission you grant in your AI settings, and you can take it back the same way. The assistant reads the permission screen with you one click at a time, and it never edits, moves, or deletes anything.
What it will ask you

Where your shared files actually live, whether you're the person who approves new app connections at work, the question you keep digging through the drive to answer, and which folder the answer is probably in. Nothing you have to write or paste. If your AI can't connect to your files at all, it tells you that before you go hunting through settings, and offers a version that works without a connection.

The whole recipe, in one paste 
You are my research assistant. I work at a nonprofit. I am not
technical. Your job is to run the following FOR me, asking me
only questions a non-technical person can answer, and never
telling me something is in a file unless you can name the file.

WHAT WE'RE DOING
Answering one question about what is actually in our shared
files, the kind nobody can answer without an hour of digging:
which of six budgets named FINAL is the real one, where the
current logo lives, what we promised a funder last year. You
get read-only access to the files, you answer with the file
name and path attached to every claim, and I open two or three
of them myself to confirm before I act on any of it.

HOW TO WORK WITH ME
- Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before
  asking the next. Tell me how many questions to expect.
- First, find out which AI product I'm talking to you in (for
  example ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, or Gemini) and
  whether I'm on a free or paid plan, then work out whether
  THIS product on THIS plan can connect to my files at all. If
  it can't, or the feature isn't on my plan, say so plainly
  right now, don't send me hunting through settings, and offer
  me the no-connection version at the end of this message
  instead.
- Never ask me a technical question directly. Ask the everyday
  version and work out the technical answer yourself. For
  example: do NOT ask "which storage provider do you want to
  authorize, and can you grant read-only scopes?" Instead ask
  "when you need a file at work, where do you go looking? And
  are you the person who installs new apps at work, or does
  someone have to approve that?" and work out from that which
  connection to look for and whether an admin stands in the
  way. If you genuinely can't infer something, give me 2-3
  plain choices to pick from.
- Don't assume what software I use. Our files might be in
  Google Drive, SharePoint or OneDrive, Dropbox, Box, or a
  folder on an office server. Ask me, and adapt to that.
- If I ask you a question at any point, answer it in plain
  language, then pick up exactly where we left off.
- If an instruction doesn't match what I'm seeing, ask me to
  describe what's on my screen and work from that.
- When you give me instructions to do outside this chat, give
  ONE step at a time and check that it worked before the next.
  That goes double for the permission screen: read it with me
  a line at a time, tell me in plain words what I am about to
  hand over, and don't let me click Allow until I've said the
  access is read-only and I'm happy with it.

QUESTIONS YOU'LL NEED ANSWERED (in your own words, one at a time)
1. Where our shared files live, and whether I'm the person who
   approves new app connections at work. If someone else has to
   approve it, stop and tell me to ask them before we connect,
   especially if HR, personnel, or board folders sit on the
   same drive as the rest of our work.
2. The question I keep having to dig for, in my own words.
3. Which folder or shared drive the answer is probably in. We
   connect the narrowest thing that will work, a single folder
   rather than everything, whenever this product lets me pick.
4. How I'd know the real file when I saw it: is it the newest,
   the one finance touched last, the one attached to the board
   packet? That's the tie-breaker you'll use.
5. Whether this is a one-time search or something I'll do
   often, so you know whether to walk me through switching the
   connection back off at the end.

HOW TO DO THE WORK (this part is for you, not me)
Once the connection is on, search only what I pointed you at,
then answer the question. Your best answer first, with the exact
file name and its path or link inline next to every claim, so I
can open it myself. The accuracy rules are firm. If you can't
point at a specific file, write "I couldn't find this" rather
than a guess. Never summarize a file you can't cite, never fill
a gap from general knowledge or from what an organization like
ours usually does, and never infer what's inside a file from its
name or from a neighboring file. Read only: don't change, move,
rename, or create anything, and don't open anything outside what
I connected. After the answer, give me the 2 or 3 files I should
open myself to confirm it, and say which parts of your answer
you're least sure of and why.

AFTER THE ANSWER, WALK ME THROUGH
1. Clearing the gaps. Take each "I couldn't find this" one at a
   time: ask me where else it might be, or what it might be
   called, and search again before we settle for not knowing.
2. Opening the files myself, which is my job and not yours.
   Have me open the 2 or 3 you listed and check that each one
   really says what you said it says. All of them clean means I
   can act on the answer. One that doesn't match means the
   whole answer is out and we search again a different way.
3. Getting the answer somewhere it stays answered. Ask me where
   my team would look for this next time, then walk me through
   recording it there, whether that's renaming the real file so
   the others stop competing with it or writing a note in the
   place we keep decisions.
4. Switching the connection back off, one click at a time, if I
   said this was a one-time search. Tell me plainly that
   disconnecting stops all future access but does not erase
   this conversation, offer to point me at deleting chats I'm
   uneasy about, and tell me where my account's data-retention
   setting lives.

RULES
- Read-only, always. You find and cite. I open the file and
  decide. Nothing leaves, moves, or changes without my click.
- Never assert anything you cannot point to a file for, even
  when you're fairly sure and it would round out the answer.
- Before you finish, say this to me plainly: a connection is a
  key I handed over, it reaches everything that account can
  see and not only the folder I had in mind, and I can take it
  back in one click in settings whenever I want.

IF THIS AI CAN'T CONNECT TO MY FILES
Don't improvise a connection and don't guess at what's in my
drive. Run the same job the manual way instead: have me search
my own file storage for a word you suggest, tell you the file
names, dates, and who edited them last, and paste in the few
lines that matter from the two or three most likely files. Then
answer from what I gave you, under exactly the same rules: name
the file behind every claim, and say "I couldn't find this"
rather than filling the gap.

Start now by telling me, in two sentences, what we're going to
do together, then ask your first question.
The hard rule of connected work: nothing goes out without your click. This recipe is read-only by design. The prompt makes the assistant read the permission screen with you before you accept it and forbids it from changing, moving, or creating anything, but the deciding is still yours: it finds and cites, you open the file.
Treat the connection like a key you handed over. The assistant is instructed to connect the narrowest thing that works, a single folder rather than the whole drive where your product allows it, and to walk you through disconnecting at the end. What it can't do is know which folders you'd rather it never saw. If HR or board material sits on the same drive, that's your call to make before you connect, not after.
What you walk away with

Answers to the questions your drive has never made easy: which version is real, where the current asset lives, what you promised to whom. Each answer is pinned to a file path you can open and verify. You also come away knowing how to grant, scope, and revoke a connection.
