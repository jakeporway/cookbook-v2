The Drive Archaeologist

Point the AI at your shared drive and ask the questions everyone actually has: which of these six "FINAL" budgets is final? Where's the current logo? What did we promise this funder last year? Read-only, every answer cites the file, and you open it yourself.

Connects to your accounts
Read-only Drive access. A permission you grant — and can take back — in your the AI settings. The AI searches and reads; it never edits, moves, or deletes your files.
Information you give it

Nothing you have to write or paste. You grant a permission: you connect Google Drive (read-only), and you can revoke it any time in settings. Then you just ask. The connection itself is the input — which is why granting it deliberately is step one of the recipe.

The recipe
  1. Switch on the Google Drive connector — and know what you just granted
    First, one org-level check: if your organization uses Google Workspace (most nonprofits do), your Workspace admin may need to approve third-party app access — ask before connecting, especially if HR or board folders live on the shared drive. In your AI chat, open Settings → Connectors and connect Google Drive. Pause at the permission screen — this is the moment that makes this a connected workflow. You're granting read access: the AI can now search and open what that account can see. It can't edit or delete anything, and you can disconnect in the same settings screen any time, in one click.
  2. Connect the narrowest thing that works
    If your setup lets you scope the connection to a specific folder — say, the shared drive you're actually digging through — do that instead of connecting everything. A folder, not the whole drive, if you can.
  3. Ask your question — with the citation rule attached
    The rule is what makes the answers trustworthy: every claim cites a file you can open yourself. Paste this, swapping in your own question.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Search my connected Google Drive and answer this question:
[We have six files with "FINAL" in the name for the FY25 budget.
Which one is actually the current, final version?]

Rules for how you answer — these are firm:
- For every claim, cite the exact file name and its path or link,
  so I can open the file myself and check.
- If you can't point to a specific file, say "I couldn't find this."
  Never summarize a file you can't cite, and never fill gaps from
  general knowledge.
- Read only. Don't change, move, rename, or create anything.

Give me your best answer first, then a short list of the
[2–3] files I should open myself to confirm.
  4. Open the cited files yourself
    The answer ends with file paths — that's on purpose. Click through and confirm before you act on anything. The AI found the trail; you make the call.
  5. When the dig is done, disconnect
    If this was a one-time excavation, go back to settings and disconnect Drive. Reconnecting later takes one click — leaving it connected should be a decision, not a leftover. Disconnecting stops all future access. It doesn't erase past conversations — delete any chats you're uneasy about, and check your account's data-retention settings once while you're in there.
What you walk away with

Answers to the questions your drive has been hiding for years — which version is real, where the current asset lives, what you promised whom — each one pinned to a file path you can open and verify yourself. Plus a connection you know how to grant, scope, and revoke.

The one hard rule of connected work: nothing goes out without your click. This recipe is read-only by design — the AI finds and cites, you open and decide. That posture is a choice you're making, and it's the right default for your first connector.
Treat the connection like a key you handed over. Connect the narrowest thing that works — a folder, not the whole drive, if you can — and remember that the AI now reads whatever it's connected to. When the archaeology is done, take the key back: disconnect in settings.
