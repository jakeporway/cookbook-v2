The Drive Archaeologist

Connect the AI to your shared drive and ask the questions that come up constantly: which of these six "FINAL" budgets is the real one, where the current logo lives, what you promised a funder last year. The access is read-only, every answer cites a file, and you open the file yourself to confirm.

Connects to your accounts
Read-only Drive access. This is a permission you grant in your AI settings, and you can take it back the same way. The AI searches and reads. It never edits, moves, or deletes your files.
Information you give it

Nothing you have to write or paste. You connect Google Drive with read-only access, which you can revoke any time in settings, and then you ask your question. The connection is the input, which is why granting it deliberately is the first step of the recipe.

The recipe
  1. Switch on the Google Drive connector and check what you granted
    Start with one org-level check. If your organization uses Google Workspace, as most nonprofits do, your Workspace admin may need to approve third-party app access. Ask before connecting, especially if HR or board folders live on the shared drive. Then, in your AI chat, open Settings → Connectors and connect Google Drive. Read the permission screen before you accept it. You're granting read access, so the AI can search and open anything that account can see. It can't edit or delete anything, and you can disconnect from the same settings screen in one click.
  2. Connect the narrowest thing that works
    If your setup lets you scope the connection to a specific folder, such as the shared drive you're actually searching, do that instead of connecting everything. Connect a single folder rather than the whole drive whenever you have the option.
  3. Ask your question, with the citation rule attached
    The citation rule is what makes the answers trustworthy: every claim has to name a file you can open yourself. Paste this, swapping in your own question.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Search my connected Google Drive and answer this question:
[We have six files with "FINAL" in the name for the FY25 budget.
Which one is actually the current, final version?]

Rules for how you answer: these are firm:
- For every claim, cite the exact file name and its path or link,
  so I can open the file myself and check.
- If you can't point to a specific file, say "I couldn't find this."
  Never summarize a file you can't cite, and never fill gaps from
  general knowledge.
- Read only. Don't change, move, rename, or create anything.

Give me your best answer first, then a short list of the
[2–3] files I should open myself to confirm.
    ROLE:
You are an archivist working in my connected Google Drive. You
are read-only, and you never assert anything you cannot point to
a file for.

GOAL:
Answer one question about what is actually in my Drive, and tell
me which files to open myself to confirm it.

CONTEXT:
The question:
[We have six files with "FINAL" in the name for the FY25
budget. Which one is actually the current, final version?]

STEPS:
1. Search my connected Google Drive for material bearing on the
   question.
2. For every claim you make, cite the exact file name and its
   path or link, so I can open the file myself and check.
3. If you can't point to a specific file, say "I couldn't find
   this." Never summarize a file you can't cite, and never fill
   gaps from general knowledge.
4. Read only. Don't change, move, rename, or create anything.

OUTPUT:
Your best answer first, with file names and links inline. Then a
short list of the [2–3] files I should open myself to confirm it.
  4. Open the cited files yourself
    The answer ends with file paths on purpose. Click through and confirm before you act on anything. The AI can find the trail, but the decision is still yours.
  5. Disconnect when you're done
    If this was a one-time search, go back to settings and disconnect Drive. Reconnecting later takes one click, so there's no cost to switching it off when you're finished. Disconnecting stops all future access, but it doesn't erase past conversations. Delete any chats you're uneasy about, and check your account's data-retention settings while you're in there.
What you walk away with

Answers to the questions your drive has never made easy: which version is real, where the current asset lives, what you promised to whom. Each answer is pinned to a file path you can open and verify. You also come away knowing how to grant, scope, and revoke a connection.

The hard rule of connected work: nothing goes out without your click. This recipe is read-only by design. The AI finds and cites; you open the file and decide what to do. That's the right default for your first connector.
Treat the connection like a key you handed over. Connect the narrowest thing that works, a single folder rather than the whole drive where you can, and remember that the AI reads whatever it's connected to. When you're finished, disconnect in settings.
