Connected · Systems

Get your data out of a system with no export button

The legacy database or portal your organization pays for holds your data but won't let you download it. An AI assistant in your browser signs in as you, works through the records while you watch, and builds the spreadsheet the vendor never gave you.

Connects to your accounts
Your own login to your own system. Not a colleague's account, and not a partner's system. Only yours.
Information you give it

Which system, which records, and which fields, plus your supervision for the whole session.

The recipe
  1. Confirm it's yours
    This recipe is for YOUR organization's data, in a system YOUR org pays for, accessed with YOUR OWN login. Not colleagues' accounts. Not systems you merely have access to through a partner.
  2. Check the boring exits first
    Before browsing, spend 15 minutes checking for an easier route. Is there an export hidden in an admin menu? Will support export on request? Is there an API? The assistant can research this for you, and a support ticket that works saves an hour of browsing.
  3. Check the vendor's terms on automated access
    Ask the assistant to find the vendor's terms and summarize what they say about automated access. Some vendors prohibit it, so read what it finds and make the decision deliberately. When in doubt, ask the vendor. You're a paying customer asking to access your own data, and that email often works.
  4. Give it the instruction, then watch
    Log in yourself in the browser extension, then give it this. It works record by record at a human pace and stops at the first surprise.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    We are exporting our organization's own records from [SYSTEM],
logged in as me, with me watching. Go record by record: open each,
copy [FIELDS] exactly as shown — never infer or reformat values.
Build a table with one row per record and the record's URL or ID
in the first column.

Pause every 20 records and show me progress. If the site shows an
error, a captcha, or anything unexpected, stop and tell me — never
retry repeatedly, and never touch settings, delete buttons, or
anything that edits data.
    ROLE:
You are operating my browser to copy out our organization's own
records. You are a careful transcriber, not an editor — you never
change anything on the site.

GOAL:
A clean table of our records from [SYSTEM], which has no export
button, with every value exactly as the system shows it.

CONTEXT:
We are exporting our organization's own records from [SYSTEM],
logged in as me, with me watching the whole time. The fields I
need are [FIELDS].

STEPS:
1. Go record by record: open each one, copy [FIELDS] exactly as
   shown — never infer, reformat, or tidy a value.
2. Record each row with the record's URL or ID in the first
   column.
3. Pause every 20 records and show me progress.
4. If the site shows an error, a captcha, or anything unexpected,
   stop and tell me. Never retry repeatedly.
5. Never touch settings, delete buttons, or anything that edits
   data. If a field is only visible in an edit view, tell me
   rather than opening it.

OUTPUT:
One table, one row per record, record URL or ID in the first
column — shown to me in batches of 20 as you go, so I can stop
you early if something looks wrong.
  5. Supervise the whole run
    Repeated rapid logins and actions can look like a break-in to the vendor's security systems. The human pace and the pauses reduce that, and your presence handles the rest.
  6. Verify, then store it properly
    Count the records and check the total against what the system reports, then spot-check 10 rows. After that, treat the export as the sensitive file it is. This is real constituent data now sitting in a spreadsheet, so store it where your org's rules say that data belongs.
What you walk away with

A verified export of your own data, built while you supervised every step and stored the way sensitive constituent data is supposed to be stored. It isn't a workaround you have to hide from your own IT policy.

Three rules, none of them flexible. It's your data and your login. You watch every minute, so this never runs unattended and it stops at the first surprise. And it stays read-only: the instruction forbids touching anything that edits, and so should you. If the vendor's terms clearly prohibit automated access, take the support-ticket route instead, and ask at renewal time why there is no export button.
