The recipe
  1. Confirm it's yours
    This recipe is for YOUR organization's data, in a system YOUR org pays for, accessed with YOUR OWN login. Not colleagues' accounts. Not systems you merely have access to through a partner.
  2. Check the boring exits first
    Before browsing, spend 15 minutes: is there an export hidden in an admin menu? Does support export on request? Is there an API? The assistant can research this — a support ticket that works beats an hour of browsing.
  3. Check the vendor's terms on automated access
    Ask the assistant to find the vendor's terms and summarize what they say about automated access. Some vendors prohibit it — read what it finds and make the call deliberately. When in doubt, ask the vendor: you're a paying customer asking to access your own data, and that email often just works.
  4. Give it the instruction, then watch
    Log in yourself in the browser extension, then give it this. It works record by record at a human pace and stops at the first surprise.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    We are exporting our organization's own records from [SYSTEM],
logged in as me, with me watching. Go record by record: open each,
copy [FIELDS] exactly as shown — never infer or reformat values.
Build a table with one row per record and the record's URL or ID
in the first column.

Pause every 20 records and show me progress. If the site shows an
error, a captcha, or anything unexpected, stop and tell me — never
retry repeatedly, and never touch settings, delete buttons, or
anything that edits data.
  5. Supervise the whole run
    Repeated rapid logins and actions can look like a break-in to the vendor's security systems — the human pace and pauses reduce that, and your presence handles the rest.
  6. Verify, then store it properly
    Count the records — does the total match what the system reports? Spot-check 10 rows. Then treat the export like the sensitive file it is: this is real constituent data now living in a spreadsheet, so store it where such data belongs, per your org's rules.
What you walk away with

A verified export of your own data, built while you supervised every step, stored the way sensitive constituent data is supposed to be stored — not a workaround you have to hide from your own IT policy.

Three lines here, all bright. It's your data and your login. You watch every minute — this never runs unattended, and it stops at the first surprise. And read-only is absolute: the instruction forbids touching anything that edits, and so do you. If the vendor's terms clearly prohibit automated access, the deliberate path is the support-ticket route, plus a pointed question at renewal time about why the export button doesn't exist.

At a supervised human pace, longer than you want — probably several sessions. 2,000 records is support-ticket territory first. If that fails, do it in chunks — this month's active volunteers first — and decide whether the full history is worth extracting at all.
