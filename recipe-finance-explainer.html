Your budget-versus-actuals export becomes a plain-language summary a board member can absorb in five minutes: where the money went, what's off track and by how much, and the questions worth asking. You verify the numbers; it does the translation.

The recipe
  1. Export budget-vs-actuals
    Pull the standard budget-vs-actuals report from your accounting system, as a CSV or PDF.
  2. Remove payroll-by-person rows if present
    If the export lists salary by name, delete those rows or roll them into a single "personnel" line before it goes anywhere.
  3. Attach the file and paste this prompt
    Attach the cleaned export to a new chat, then paste this. Swap in last quarter's narrative if you have one.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Chat prompt
    Write a plain-language financial summary for a nonprofit board
using ONLY the numbers in the attached file. Never compute a
number that isn't derivable from it, and cite the line item for
every figure you mention.

Structure it as:
1. Overall position, in two sentences.
2. The 3-5 variances that matter most — for each, the budget
   figure, the actual figure, and the difference.
3. Anything trending toward a problem if the pattern continues.
4. Three questions a board member should ask.

If a row is ambiguous, flag it and say why — don't interpret it
for me. [Optional: match the tone of this prior board narrative:
PASTE PRIOR NARRATIVE]
  4. Check every number against the export
    The citations make this a 10-minute job, not an afternoon — line up each cited figure against the source file.
  5. Rewrite the judgment calls in your own words
    The AI knows arithmetic, not your context. "The variance is a timing issue with the state contract" is knowledge only you have — add it yourself.
  6. ED reviews before the packet goes out
    A final read from the person accountable for the numbers, before the summary joins the board packet.
What you walk away with

A cited, plain-language draft of the financial narrative section — the kind of translation that used to take an afternoon of writing and rewriting, ready for your own judgment calls and the ED's sign-off.

The numbers are yours to verify, and the interpretation is yours to own. This recipe translates; it does not analyze strategy. A variance explanation you can't personally defend to the board doesn't go in the summary.
