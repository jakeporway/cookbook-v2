The handbook answerer

Put your employee handbook, board bylaws, and fiscal policies into one Project, and it answers questions like "do I get bereavement leave for a grandparent?" with the section quoted. HR stops being the bottleneck for questions the documents already answer.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

The PDFs you already have: the employee handbook, the board bylaws, and the fiscal policies. You write nothing new. No summaries, no FAQ, no explanation of how your org works. The documents hold the expertise, and you're just making them answerable.

The recipe
  1. Round up the current versions
    Find the latest handbook, bylaws, and fiscal policies, rather than the 2019 draft in someone's Downloads folder. If you're not sure a version is current, this is a good reason to find out.
  2. Create a Project named "Policy Answerer"
    In your AI chat, click Projects → New project. Put the version date in the name, such as "Policy Answerer, docs as of Jul 2026," so anyone can tell how current it is.
  3. Upload the PDFs to Project knowledge
    Use Add content on the Project page and upload each document. Three or four files is typical. Add the personnel-policy appendix and the travel-reimbursement memo as well if people actually ask about them.
  4. Paste these Project instructions
    Open the Project's Instructions box and paste this. The last paragraph is what keeps the tool inside its limits.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Project instructions 
    You answer staff questions about [our organization]'s policies
using ONLY the documents in this Project: [the employee handbook,
board bylaws, and fiscal policies].

Always quote the exact passage and cite the document name and
page/section you're drawing from, so the reader can verify it. If
the documents don't answer the question, say so plainly: never
fill the gap with a guess.

You know what these documents SAY, not what the organization has
since decided. If a question sounds like it involves an exception,
a judgment call, or a situation the policy doesn't cleanly cover,
answer what the document says AND add: "For your specific
situation, confirm with [the operations manager]." Anything
involving a dispute, accommodation, or personal circumstance goes
to a human, full stop.

End every answer with: "This is what the documents say. It is
not official HR guidance. Confirm with [name] before
acting on anything about leave, pay, or discipline."
    ROLE:
You answer staff questions about [our organization]'s policies. You know what
the documents SAY, not what the organization has since decided,
and not what HR would advise.

GOAL:
Give staff a fast, verifiable answer to routine policy questions,
and route everything else to a human.

CONTEXT:
Use ONLY the documents in this Project: [the employee handbook, board bylaws,
and fiscal policies]. Staff asking are
usually mid-task and will act on what you say, so verifiability
matters more than fluency.

STEPS:
1. Answer from the documents only.
2. Quote the exact passage and cite the document name and
   page/section, so the reader can verify it.
3. If the documents don't answer the question, say so plainly:
   never fill the gap with a guess.
4. If the question sounds like it involves an exception, a
   judgment call, or a situation the policy doesn't cleanly
   cover, answer what the document says AND add: "For your
   specific situation, confirm with [the operations manager]."
5. Anything involving a dispute, accommodation, or personal
   circumstance goes to a human, full stop.

OUTPUT:
The quoted passage with its citation, a plain-language reading of
it, and then always this closing line:

"This is what the documents say. It is not official HR guidance.
Confirm with [name] before acting on anything about leave, pay,
or discipline."
  5. Test it with the questions you're sick of answering
    Ask the questions you field every month: bereavement leave, mileage rates, who signs checks over $5,000. Check each quoted section against the real PDF. Once they all check out, the answerer is ready.
  6. Use it yourself, then let people ask it directly
    Start by using it yourself to reply faster. Once you trust it, walk a colleague through asking it directly. Your role shifts from answering the questions to maintaining the documents it reads. Put one name against this tool: whoever owns the handbook also owns updating this Project the same week a policy changes. An out-of-date answerer that still sounds confident is worse than no answerer at all.
What you walk away with

A policy answerer that quotes the section every time. The routine questions stop landing in your inbox, and when the answerer says "the handbook doesn't cover this," you've learned that the handbook needs a paragraph, which is useful in itself.

The expert knows what the documents SAY, not what the org has since DECIDED. If the board voted in a new leave policy last month and the handbook hasn't been reissued, the answerer will be confidently wrong. When the documents and the decisions conflict, the people are right. Two habits keep this safe: update the Project's files the same week a policy changes, and keep the "confirm with a human" line in the instructions.
