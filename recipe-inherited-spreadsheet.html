Your predecessor left a workbook with 11 tabs, colored cells, and formulas nobody understands — and then left the org. Hand it over and get a plain-language map: what each tab is for, what calculates what, and which cells are load-bearing.

Information you give it

The workbook itself — "FY24 MASTER TRACKER v7 (USE THIS ONE).xlsx", exactly as your predecessor left it. Or better, a copy with the dollar figures blanked out: the tabs, formulas, and colored cells are what need explaining, and those aren't the sensitive part. You point at the artifact; you don't have to describe it.

The recipe
  1. Make a copy, and decide what to blank
    Save a copy of the workbook first — you'll attach the copy, never the original. If it holds donor gifts, salaries, or client info, select those value columns in the copy and delete the contents (leave the headers and formulas). The skeleton is what you need explained. Not sure whether deleting a number will break a formula? Don't touch it — attach the file as-is and just tell the AI: "the dollar figures are sensitive; explain the structure but don't repeat the numbers back to me."
  2. Open a chat and attach the copy
    Use the paperclip, same as attaching to an email. This is the moment the file leaves your machine — which is why it's the blanked copy.
  3. Ask for the map
    Paste this, with one sentence of context about what the workbook is supposed to track.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    I inherited this workbook from someone who has left the
organization, and nobody here fully understands it. It's supposed
to track [our fundraising pipeline and gift entry for FY24].
I've blanked some values — please explain the structure, not the
numbers.

Give me a plain-language map, assuming I'm comfortable with
spreadsheets but new to THIS one:

1. One paragraph per tab: what it's for and whether anything
   else depends on it.
2. How the tabs feed each other — which ones I type into, and
   which ones calculate themselves.
3. The load-bearing cells: the ones where a careless edit would
   quietly break totals elsewhere. Tell me exactly where they
   are (tab and cell) and what they do.
4. Anything that looks broken, orphaned, or suspicious — formula
   errors, tabs nothing references, a hidden sheet.
5. What the colors and any cryptic column headers seem to mean —
   and say plainly when you're guessing.

I'll ask follow-ups about specific cells after I read this.
  4. Interrogate it while the file is still in view
    This is the good part. "What happens if I add a row to the Gifts tab?" "Why is cell C47 red?" "Which tab does the board report actually pull from?" Ask everything you were embarrassed to ask a human.
  5. Save the map into the workbook itself
    Paste the explanation into a new first tab called "READ ME — how this works." Verify the two or three most load-bearing claims yourself before you rely on them. You've just ended the cycle for the next person.
This file does go to the chat. That's what makes this recipe amber, not green. If the workbook holds donor gifts, salaries, or anything about the people you serve, blank those values in your copy first — the structure is what needs explaining, and the structure survives with the numbers gone. When in doubt, blank it out.
What you walk away with

A plain-language owner's manual for a workbook that used to live in one departed person's head — which tabs to touch, which cells never to touch, and what's quietly broken. Plus a READ ME tab so nobody at your org has to inherit a mystery again.
