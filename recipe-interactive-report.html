Turn a report into an interactive web page

For the audiences who will never open a PDF, your annual report or program overview becomes a single web page you send as a link: scrollable, readable on a phone, with your charts rendered live.

Public-facing output
The public sees this. Someone reviews every word before the link goes out, the same as with any publication. Nothing here skips your normal sign-off.
Information you give it

The finished report as a PDF, your logo, and one existing page of yours whose look you like. A screenshot of that page is fine. There's no need to rewrite the report first.

The recipe
  1. Attach the report, your logo, and a page whose look you like
    Open a normal chat and attach the finished report as a PDF, your logo file, and a screenshot of one existing page: your website, a past newsletter, or anything whose colors and feel you want matched.
  2. Ask for a single self-contained page
    Paste this. The "keep every fact exact" line is the most important sentence in the prompt, and it's what keeps the review step in the next minute quick.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Copy it exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Turn this report into a single self-contained HTML page I can send
as a link.

Keep every fact and number exactly as written, do not summarize,
rewrite, or add anything. Match the colors and feel of the attached
screenshot. Make it readable on a phone. Keep the sections in the
same order as the report. Render the key numbers as simple charts.
    ROLE:
You are building a web version of a report someone else wrote.
You are a designer and typesetter, not an editor.

GOAL:
A single self-contained HTML page carrying the whole report, that
I can send as a link.

CONTEXT:
The report is attached, along with a screenshot showing the
colors and feel to match. Most people will open the link on a
phone.

STEPS:
1. Keep every fact and number exactly as written, do not
   summarize, rewrite, condense, or add anything.
2. Keep the sections in the same order as the report.
3. Match the colors and feel of the attached screenshot.
4. Render the key numbers as simple charts, built from the
   report's own figures.
5. Make it readable on a phone: real text sizes, nothing that
   requires pinching.

OUTPUT:
One self-contained HTML file, no external files or fonts needed,
plus a one-line note on anything from the report you could not
represent cleanly on the page.
  3. Open the file and check every number
    Double-click the file the AI hands back. Go section by section and check every number against the source report. The no-new-words rule in the prompt makes this quick, because there's nothing new to fact-check, only matching to confirm.
  4. Fix it with specific requests, and stop after two rounds
    If something's off, ask for the exact change, such as "make the third chart a bar chart instead of a pie," rather than "make it better." Give yourself two rounds of revisions and stop there. A page that's 90% right after two rounds is ready to publish.
  5. Put it at a real link
    You can host the page free on GitHub Pages or Netlify Drop, or simply attach the HTML file to an email, since it opens in any browser. Ask the AI for click-by-click instructions for whichever option you pick.
What you walk away with

A single link that opens the same report on any phone or laptop: scrollable, on-brand, and checked number by number against the source. No PDF viewer required.

The risk here isn't the code, it's drift in the content. A "helpful" rewrite of your numbers is the failure that matters. The keep-every-fact-exact instruction, plus your line-by-line check, is what makes this publishable. Nothing goes to the public unreviewed.
