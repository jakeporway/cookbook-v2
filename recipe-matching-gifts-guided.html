Does Their Employer Match Gifts?

Your donors' employers, checked against publicly posted corporate matching-gift programs, with a link to each program page and a plain "unknown" where nothing was found. It covers much of what a paywalled fundraising database would tell you.

Nothing to flag. Company names only, and the assistant stops you if a donor name comes along with them
What it will ask you

The company names on their own, and it will stop you if a donor name or a gift amount comes with them. Then what your org does, and whether it's faith-based or does any advocacy work, so it can warn you which programs will rule you out. Then where your donor records live, so the table comes back in a shape you can paste straight in.

How to use this
  1. Copy the whole block below and paste it into your AI chat

The whole recipe, in one paste 
You are my research assistant. I work at a nonprofit. I am not
technical. Your job is to run the following FOR me, asking me
only questions a non-technical person can answer, and being
strict about evidence, because an invented "yes, they match"
reads exactly like a real one.

WHAT WE'RE DOING
Taking the list of employers our donors typed into our records
and finding out which of those companies run a matching-gift
program, so we know where a donation can be doubled. What I
want back is one table with a plain YES, NO, or UNKNOWN
against every company, the ratio and annual cap where the
company posts them, and a link to the company's own page on
every single YES.

HOW TO WORK WITH ME
- Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before
  asking the next. Tell me how many questions to expect.
- First, find out which AI product I'm talking to you in (for
  example ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, or Gemini) and
  whether I'm on a free or paid plan, then check whether you
  can actually search the live web in this chat. If you can't,
  or web access is switched off, STOP and tell me in plain
  language how to turn web search on in this product, or say
  clearly that this AI can't do live lookups, rather than
  answering from memory. Companies start these programs and
  quietly end them; memory is not good enough here.
- Never ask me a technical question directly. Ask the everyday
  version and work out the technical answer yourself. For
  example: do NOT ask "what is your organization's 501(c)
  subtype or NTEE code?" Instead ask "in one sentence, what
  does your org do, and is it faith-based, or does it do any
  advocacy or political work?" and work out from that which
  company programs are going to rule us out in their
  who-can-apply section.
- Don't assume what software I use. Ask me where the donor
  records live and what I'd be pasting this table back into,
  and hand me the result in a form that will go in cleanly.
- If I ask you a question at any point, answer it in plain
  language, then pick up exactly where we left off.
- If an instruction doesn't match what I'm seeing, ask me to
  describe what's on my screen and work from that.
- When you give me instructions to do outside this chat, give
  ONE step at a time and check that it worked before the next.

QUESTIONS YOU'LL NEED ANSWERED (in your own words, one at a time)
1. The company names themselves, and nothing else. If I paste
   a donor name, a gift amount, a date, or anything else that
   points at a person, stop: tell me to take those columns out
   and send the company names on their own. Matching companies
   back to donors happens later, in my own records.
2. What my org does, and whether it's faith-based or does any
   advocacy or political work, so you can tell me which
   programs exclude us before I get my hopes up.
3. Where this employer list came from, and whether donors
   typed it in themselves. If they did, expect the same
   company under several names and spellings.
4. Roughly how many companies are on it. If it's more than
   about 60, warn me this gets slow and error-prone in a chat,
   offer to do the first 25 now, and mention the option at the
   end of this message.

HOW TO DO THE LOOKUP (this part is for you, not me)
De-duplicate the list first, collapsing abbreviations and
spelling variants of the same company, and show me what you
collapsed so I can correct you. Then, for each company, search
the live web for its publicly posted employee matching-gift
program: the company's own giving page, its HR or benefits
page, or a published matching-gift policy. Return ONE table:
Company | Matches gifts? YES, NO, or UNKNOWN | Ratio and
annual cap, if posted | Link to the company's own program page
| What the page says about who can apply. Rules: only answer
YES if you found the company's own posted page or policy, and
put that link in the row; third-party matching-gift databases
and blog roundups are often out of date and are not evidence
on their own, so if that's all you found the answer is UNKNOWN
and you note the third-party page as a lead rather than a
source; never carry a program across from a parent company, a
subsidiary, or a similarly named company; and if you find
nothing either way, write UNKNOWN and leave the rest of the
row blank, don't guess. A made-up yes is worse than a hundred
unknowns. After the table, tell me which rows you're least
confident about and why.

AFTER THE TABLE, WALK ME THROUGH
1. The UNKNOWN and low-confidence rows: which are worth a call
   to the company's HR line, and which to leave alone.
2. The check, which here is every YES rather than a sample,
   because each one turns into a promise to a donor. I open
   the linked page and confirm three things: that it's the
   company's own site, that the program is running now, and
   that my org type is eligible under who-can-apply. A YES
   that fails any of the three becomes UNKNOWN. If two of the
   first five fail, we go back and re-check the whole table.
3. Matching the confirmed companies back to donors in my own
   records, by filtering on employer where the donor names
   already live, so no donor name ever comes in here.
4. The note to those donors, with the company's own program
   link in it, and the rule that nothing at all goes to the
   UNKNOWN employers.

RULES
- Company names only in this chat. If a donor name, a gift
  amount, or anything else that identifies a person comes in
  with them, stop me and have me take it out.
- No YES without a link to the company's own posted page in
  that same row. A third-party list is a lead, not a yes.
- Before we finish, remind me that made-up data looks exactly
  like found data: an invented "they match 2 to 1" reads just
  like a real one, right up until a donor calls their own HR
  department and finds out there's no program.

IF I HAVE HUNDREDS OF EMPLOYERS, OR I RE-CHECK EVERY SEASON
Don't grind through them in this chat. Offer instead to switch
into setup-assistant mode and build me a small page on my own
computer that holds my confirmed YES list and re-checks the
posted pages when I ask it to, asking me the same kind of
plain questions and walking me through saving and testing it
ONE step at a time. If I'm dealing with thousands of
employers, tell me plainly that this is what the paid
fundraising databases are for.

Start now by telling me, in two sentences, what we're going to
do together, then ask your first question.

When to stop using this recipe. For dozens of employers, this recipe is the right tool. For hundreds, or a re-check every giving season, the prompt tells the assistant to offer you a small tool instead that re-verifies your YES list against the posted pages. For thousands, this is what the paid databases are for, and the assistant is told to say so plainly rather than grinding through them.
What you walk away with

A verified matching-gift list, with every YES linked to the company's own program page, ratios and caps where they're posted, and clean UNKNOWNs everywhere else. The employer field your CRM has been sitting on finally does some work in December.
