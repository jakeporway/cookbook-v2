Does Their Employer Match Gifts?

Your donors' employers, checked against publicly posted corporate matching-gift programs, with a link to each program page and a plain "unknown" where nothing was found. It covers much of what a paywalled fundraising database would tell you.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

The employer column from your donor records, which is the field nobody has looked at since the donors filled it in. Company names only, with no donor names and no gift amounts.

The recipe
  1. Export the employer column, alone
    From your CRM or spreadsheet, copy just the company names. De-duplicate them if that's easy to do. If not, the AI will handle it in the next step.
  2. Paste the companies with this prompt
    The rule that matters most is that a YES requires the company's own posted page. Everything else is UNKNOWN.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Here is the employer column from our donor list — company
names only, no donor names:

[paste your company list]

De-duplicate it, then for each company, search the web for
its publicly posted employee matching-gift program — the
company's own giving page, HR benefits page, or published
matching-gift policy.

Return one table: Company | Matches gifts? (YES / NO /
UNKNOWN) | Match ratio & annual cap, if posted | Link to
the company's own program page.

Only answer YES if you found the company's own posted page
or policy — and link it in that row. Third-party lists and
blog posts don't count as a YES on their own. If you find
nothing either way, write UNKNOWN and leave the rest of the
row blank — do not guess. A made-up "yes" is worse than a
hundred unknowns.
    ROLE:
You are a prospect researcher. You only report what a company
itself has published, and you would rather return a hundred
unknowns than one invented yes.

GOAL:
Find out which of our donors' employers run a matching-gift
program, so we know where a donation can be doubled.

CONTEXT:
Here is the employer column from our donor list — company names
only, no donor names:

[paste your company list]

Third-party matching-gift databases and blog roundups are often
out of date; they don't count as evidence on their own.

STEPS:
1. De-duplicate the company list first.
2. For each company, search the web for its publicly posted employee matching-gift program — the
   company's own giving page, HR benefits page, or published
   matching-gift policy.
3. Only answer YES if you found the company's own posted page or
   policy, and link it in that row.
4. If you find nothing either way, write UNKNOWN and leave the
   rest of the row blank. Do not guess. A made-up "yes" is worse
   than a hundred unknowns.

OUTPUT:
One table: Company | Matches gifts? (YES / NO / UNKNOWN) | Match
ratio & annual cap, if posted | Link to the company's own program
page.
  3. Click through every YES before you use it
    Open the linked page and confirm three things: that it's the company's own site, that the program is current, and that your org type is eligible. Some programs exclude religious or political orgs, so check the "who can apply" or eligibility section, which is usually one short paragraph. Do this for all the YESes rather than a sample. The verifying is the actual work here, so leave time for it.
  4. Match the YES companies back to donors on your own machine
    Back in your own spreadsheet, filter donors by the confirmed employers. The donor names never entered the chat, since the matching happens in your CRM where they already live.
  5. Send the "your gift could double" note
    Include the company's own program link in the email. The donor files the paperwork and your org gets the match. Send nothing to the UNKNOWN employers, because a wrong "your employer matches!" costs you a donor's trust.
What you walk away with

A verified matching-gift list, with every YES linked to the company's own program page, ratios and caps where they're posted, and clean UNKNOWNs everywhere else. The employer field your CRM has been sitting on finally does some work in December.

Made-up data looks exactly like found data. An invented "yes, they match 2:1" reads exactly like a real one, right up until a donor calls HR and finds out there's no program. So: a source link on every YES, unknowns left as UNKNOWN, and a click through every YES before anyone acts on it.
When to stop using this recipe. For dozens of employers, this recipe is the right tool. For hundreds, or a re-check every giving season, ask the AI to build a small tool that re-verifies your YES list against the posted pages. For thousands, this is what the paid databases are for.
