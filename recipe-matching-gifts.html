Does Their Employer Match Gifts?

Your donors' employers, checked against publicly posted corporate matching-gift programs — with a link to each program page and a plain "unknown" where nothing was found. The free version of a paywalled fundraising database.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

The employer column from your donor records — the field nobody has looked at since the donors filled it in. Company names only; no donor names, no gift amounts.

The recipe
  1. Export the employer column, alone
    From your CRM or spreadsheet, copy just the company names. De-duplicate if it's easy; if not, the AI will do it in the next step.
  2. Paste the companies with this prompt
    The one rule that matters most: YES requires the company's own posted page. Everything else is UNKNOWN.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Here is the employer column from our donor list — company
names only, no donor names:

[paste your company list]

De-duplicate it, then for each company, search the web for
its publicly posted employee matching-gift program — the
company's own giving page, HR benefits page, or published
matching-gift policy.

Return one table: Company | Matches gifts? (YES / NO /
UNKNOWN) | Match ratio & annual cap, if posted | Link to
the company's own program page.

Only answer YES if you found the company's own posted page
or policy — and link it in that row. Third-party lists and
blog posts don't count as a YES on their own. If you find
nothing either way, write UNKNOWN and leave the rest of the
row blank — do not guess. A made-up "yes" is worse than a
hundred unknowns.
  3. Click through every YES before you use it
    Open the linked page and confirm it's the company's own site, the program is current, and your org type is eligible (some programs exclude religious or political orgs — check the "who can apply" or eligibility section of the program page; it's usually one short paragraph). This is the spot-check — do it for all the YESes, not a sample, because the verifying IS the task — budget for it.
  4. Match the YES companies back to donors — on your machine
    Back in your own spreadsheet, filter donors by the confirmed employers. The donor names never entered the chat; the join happens in your CRM, where they already live.
  5. Send the "your gift could double" note
    Include the company's own program link in the email — the donor files the paperwork, you get the match. UNKNOWN employers get nothing; a wrong "your employer matches!" costs you a donor's trust.
What you walk away with

A verified matching-gift list — every YES linked to the company's own program page, ratios and caps where posted, and clean UNKNOWNs everywhere else. The employer field your CRM has been sitting on, finally earning its keep in December.

Made-up data looks exactly like found data. A confabulated "yes, they match 2:1" reads exactly like a real one — until a donor calls HR and finds out there's no program. The rule: a source link per YES, unknowns stay UNKNOWN, and click through every YES before anyone acts on it.
The lookup line. Dozens of employers → this recipe. Hundreds, or a re-check every giving season → ask the AI to build a small tool that re-verifies your YES list against the posted pages. Thousands → you've outgrown the recipe; that's what the paid databases are for.
