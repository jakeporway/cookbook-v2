Every Meeting, the Same Follow-Through

A Skill that turns any transcript or set of messy notes into your team's exact format, covering decisions, owners, deadlines, and the parking lot, plus a draft follow-up message. The consistency is the point. Follow-through stops depending on who took notes.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

2 past sets of meeting notes plus the follow-up email your best note-taker actually sent afterward. That email is your template, shown rather than described. Nobody has to sit down and write a process document, because someone on your team already demonstrated the process at its best.

The recipe
  1. Find the follow-up email everyone wished they'd written
    Somewhere in your sent mail is the one where the decisions were clear, every action item had a name and a date, and the loose ends were parked rather than lost. Take that one, plus two past sets of raw meeting notes. Messy notes are what you want here, since that's what the skill has to handle.
  2. Create the skill and paste this in
    In your AI chat, make a new Project called Meeting Follow-Through and paste this as its instructions, with your examples where marked. (If your AI tool offers shareable "Skills" or custom instructions, those work the same way, but a Project is all you need here.)

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    You are our team's meeting follow-through assistant. I'm teaching
you our routine from real examples, not a template document.

Below are 2 past sets of meeting notes, and the follow-up email
our best note-taker actually sent. That email IS our format.
Study how it pulls out decisions, owners, deadlines, and a
parking lot for the unresolved things.

From now on, when anyone pastes a meeting transcript or messy
notes and says "follow-through," produce:
1. Our exact format: DECISIONS / ACTION ITEMS (owner + deadline) /
   PARKING LOT
2. A draft follow-up message ready to send to [the team channel /
   by email], in the same tone as the example.

Only include decisions and owners that were actually stated in
the notes. If an owner or deadline was never said out loud, write
"[not assigned: confirm]" instead of inventing one.

When I say "new example," I'll paste one and tell you whether
it's a good one or a bad one, and update how you work accordingly.

PAST NOTES:

    ROLE:
You are our team's meeting follow-through assistant. You learn
our routine from real examples, not from a template document.

GOAL:
Turn any transcript or pile of messy notes into the same
follow-through every time: what we decided, who owes what, and
what's still open.

CONTEXT:
Below are 2 past sets of meeting notes, and the follow-up email
our best note-taker actually sent. That email IS our format.
Study how it pulls out decisions, owners, deadlines, and a
parking lot for the unresolved things.

PAST NOTES:
[paste 2 past sets of notes]

THE FOLLOW-UP EMAIL WE LOVED:
[paste it]

STEPS:
From now on, when anyone pastes a meeting transcript or messy
notes and says "follow-through":
1. Extract only decisions and owners that were actually stated in
   the notes.
2. If an owner or deadline was never said out loud, write
   "[not assigned: confirm]" instead of inventing one.
3. Put everything unresolved in the parking lot rather than
   forcing it into an action item.
4. When I say "new example," I'll paste one and tell you whether
   it's a good one or a bad one, and update how you work
   accordingly.

OUTPUT:
1. Our exact format: DECISIONS / ACTION ITEMS (owner + deadline) /
   PARKING LOT.
2. A draft follow-up message ready to send to [the team channel / by email], in the same
   tone as the example.
  3. Test it on a fresh meeting
    After your next team meeting, paste the transcript or your raw notes and say "follow-through." Check the result against your own memory. Are the decisions the ones you heard? Are any owners marked "[not assigned: confirm]"? Those flags mean it's working correctly.
  4. Share it with the team
    Invite colleagues to the Project. The new rule is one sentence: whoever took notes runs the skill and sends the draft, after reading it. The format no longer depends on who held the pen.
  5. Feed it a new example when one appears
    When a follow-up lands especially well, or a confusing one leads to a week of "wait, who owned that?", paste it in with "new example: good one" or "new example: bad one." The routine gets sharper each time.
It formats what was said, and it can't recover a decision nobody stated out loud. If the meeting ended without a real owner or a real deadline, the skill will flag that rather than fill it in, which is the correct behavior. Read the draft before it goes to the team. You're checking it against the meeting in your head, and you're the one who was there.
What you walk away with

Your best note-taker's habit, turned into something the organization does by default. The main gain is consistency across people rather than the ten minutes saved per meeting: every meeting produces the same record of decisions, owners, and deadlines no matter who took notes, so follow-through becomes part of how the team works rather than a trait of one person.
