Every Meeting, the Same Follow-Through

A Skill that turns any transcript or messy notes into your team's exact format — decisions, owners, deadlines, parking lot — plus a draft follow-up message. The consistency is the point: follow-through stops depending on who took notes.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

2 past sets of meeting notes plus the follow-up email your best note-taker actually sent afterward. That email is your template, demonstrated instead of described — you never sit down to write a process doc, because someone on your team already showed the process at its best.

The recipe
  1. Find the follow-up email everyone wished they'd written
    Somewhere in your sent mail is the one where the decisions were clear, every action item had a name and a date, and the loose ends were parked, not lost. Grab it, plus two past sets of raw meeting notes (messy is fine — messy is the point).
  2. Create the skill and paste this in
    In your AI chat, make a new Project called Meeting Follow-Through (If your AI tool offers "Skills" or custom instructions you can share, that's the same idea one step up — but a Project is all you need here.) and paste this as its instructions, with your examples where marked.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    You are our team's meeting follow-through assistant. I'm teaching
you our routine from real examples, not a template document.

Below are 2 past sets of meeting notes, and the follow-up email
our best note-taker actually sent. That email IS our format —
study how it pulls out decisions, owners, deadlines, and a
parking lot for the unresolved things.

From now on, when anyone pastes a meeting transcript or messy
notes and says "follow-through," produce:
1. Our exact format: DECISIONS / ACTION ITEMS (owner + deadline) /
   PARKING LOT
2. A draft follow-up message ready to send to [the team channel /
   by email], in the same tone as the example.

Only include decisions and owners that were actually stated in
the notes. If an owner or deadline was never said out loud, write
"[not assigned — confirm]" instead of inventing one.

When I say "new example," I'll paste one and tell you whether
it's a good one or a bad one — update how you work accordingly.

PAST NOTES:
[paste 2 past sets of notes]

THE FOLLOW-UP EMAIL WE LOVED:
[paste it]
  3. Test it on a fresh meeting
    After your next team meeting, paste the transcript or your raw notes and say "follow-through." Check it against your own memory: are the decisions the ones you heard? Are any owners marked "[not assigned — confirm]"? Those flags are it doing its job.
  4. Share it with the team
    Invite colleagues to the Project. New rule, one sentence long: whoever took notes runs the skill and sends the draft — after reading it. Now the format doesn't depend on who held the pen.
  5. Feed it a new example when one appears
    When a follow-up lands especially well — or a confusing one causes a week of "wait, who owned that?" — paste it in with "new example: good one" or "new example: bad one." The routine keeps tightening.
It formats what was said — it can't recover a decision nobody stated out loud. If the meeting ended without a real owner or a real deadline, the skill will (correctly) flag it, not fill it in. Read the draft before it goes to the team: you're checking it against the meeting in your head, and you're the only one who was there.
What you walk away with

Your best note-taker's habit, turned into an org function. The payoff isn't the ten minutes saved per meeting — it's consistency across people: every meeting produces the same decisions-owners-deadlines record no matter who took notes, so follow-through stops being a personality trait and becomes how the team works.
