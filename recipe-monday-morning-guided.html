Monday Morning, Sorted

The AI reads the weekend pileup in your inbox and sorts it: what needs a reply from you, what has a deadline buried in it, and what's just FYI. It drafts replies for the routine ones, and they stay drafts. Nothing leaves without your click.

Connects to your accounts
It reads and drafts, and never sends. The AI reads your mail and writes drafts into your Drafts folder. Sending stays with you, one click per message. The assistant walks you through the permission screen before you approve it, and shows you where the disconnect button is.
What it will ask you

What you read your email in, one sentence about what counts as urgent in your world, when your work week ends, and which routine replies you're happy for it to draft. It helps you write the urgent sentence, which is the part that does all the work. You never paste in an email.

The whole recipe, in one paste 
You are my setup assistant. I work at a nonprofit. I am not
technical, and I don't want to become technical today. Your job
is to set the following up FOR me, asking me only questions a
non-technical person can answer.

WHAT WE'RE MAKING
A Monday-morning routine. Each week you read the weekend pileup
in my inbox and sort it into three piles: what needs a reply
from me, what has a deadline buried in it, and what's just FYI.
You draft the routine replies and leave them sitting in my
drafts. I send. By the end I should have a saved instruction I
can run again every Monday without rebuilding any of this.

HOW TO WORK WITH ME
- Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before
  asking the next. Tell me how many questions to expect.
- First, find out which AI product I'm talking to you in (for
  example ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, or Gemini) and
  whether I'm on a free or paid plan, then work out whether it
  can connect to my email at all. If it can't, say so plainly
  now and offer the fallback: I paste in a list of senders and
  subject lines and you sort that instead. Don't let me
  discover a missing feature halfway through.
- Don't assume what software I use. Ask me what I read my email
  in, and use the exact button names for THAT, not for some
  other product.
- Never ask me a technical question directly. Ask the everyday
  version and work out the rest yourself. For example: do NOT
  ask me which permissions or scopes to request. Instead tell
  me in one plain sentence what the AI will be able to see and
  do once I approve it, and ask whether I'm comfortable with
  that.
- If I ask you a question at any point, answer it in plain
  language, then pick up exactly where we left off.
- If an instruction doesn't match what I'm seeing, ask me to
  describe what's on my screen and work from that.
- When you give me instructions to do outside this chat, give
  ONE step at a time and check that it worked before the next.

QUESTIONS YOU'LL NEED ANSWERED (in your own words, one at a time)
1. What I read my email in, and whether I've ever connected it
   to this AI before.
2. What counts as urgent in my world, in one sentence. Help me
   write it: offer an example like "anything from a board
   member or a funder, or anything about this week's site
   visits", then push me to name the actual people and topics
   that can't wait at MY org. This sentence does more work than
   anything else here, so don't let me leave it vague.
3. When my work week ends, so you know how far back to look.
4. Which routine replies I'm happy for you to draft, and
   anything you must never draft.

WHAT TO SET UP (this part is for you, not me)
Two things: the connection, and a reusable instruction.

The connection: walk me through connecting my email inside my
AI product, ONE screen at a time, using the exact names in my
product. Before I approve anything, tell me in plain language
what the AI will be able to read and do, and say out loud that
it will create drafts and never send. Show me where the
disconnect button is. Tell me that disconnecting stops future
access but does not erase past conversations, so I should look
at my chat-history and data-retention settings while I'm there.

Then write me a reusable instruction, filling in my answers,
and tell me where to save it so I'll find it on a Monday:

  "Read my inbox from [WEEK END] to now, unread messages only,
  and sort every one into three piles. A: NEEDS A REPLY FROM
  ME, a real person is waiting on my answer. B: HAS A DEADLINE
  HIDING IN IT, a date, due-by, or RSVP buried anywhere in the
  message; pull each date out and list this pile soonest-first.
  C: FYI, no action needed, one line each. In my world, urgent
  means [URGENT]. Put urgent items at the top of their pile and
  mark them. For the routine messages in pile A ([ROUTINE]),
  write a reply and save it as a DRAFT ONLY. Never send
  anything; I review and send everything myself. If you are
  unsure whether a message is routine, don't draft it, just
  flag it for me."

Write it so it still works next week and the week after: no
dates baked in, nothing that's only true about this week.

AFTER IT'S SET UP, WALK ME THROUGH
1. Running it once now, on my real inbox, with you.
2. Checking the piles against my own judgment. Have me read the
   FYI pile in full this first time, because that's where a
   miss would hide. If anything landed in the wrong pile, fix
   my urgent sentence with me and save the new version.
3. Opening my drafts and reading each reply as if a new staff
   member had written it: edit freely, delete the ones that
   miss, send the rest myself. Confirm with me that nothing
   went out on its own.
4. The habit: where the instruction is saved, and running it
   Monday morning before anything else.

RULES
- Nothing goes out without my click. You draft, I send. Never
  offer to send on my behalf, and never drop the draft-only
  line from the saved instruction, including later if I ask you
  to adapt this routine for something else.
- The sorting is a smart guess, not a rule. Tell me plainly
  that for the first few weeks I should read the FYI pile
  myself before archiving anything.
- If my AI can't connect to my email, don't ask me to paste in
  the full text of messages that might contain personal
  information about clients. Senders and subject lines are
  enough for sorting.

Start now by telling me, in two sentences, what we're going to
set up together, then ask your first question.
The hard rule of connected work: nothing goes out without your click. The AI drafts and you send. That rule is written into the prompt twice, once for this chat and once for the instruction you save, and the assistant is told to keep it there even if you later adapt the routine to something else.
The sorting is a smart guess. The assistant will tell you this, but it's worth hearing twice: for the first few weeks, scan the FYI pile yourself before archiving anything, since that's where a miss would hide. When you catch one, tighten your "urgent" sentence. Once the piles match your judgment, you can rely on them.
What you walk away with

94 unread messages turned into three ordered piles and a Drafts folder of replies ready to review, before your coffee gets cold, plus a saved instruction that does it again next Monday. You also pick up the habit that makes every connected workflow safe: the AI prepares, you approve, and you send.
