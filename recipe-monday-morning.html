Connected Workflow · Triage

Monday Morning, Sorted

The AI reads your weekend inbox pileup and hands you a triage: what needs a reply from you, what has a deadline hiding in it, what's FYI. It drafts replies for the routine ones — drafts only. Nothing leaves without your click.

Connects to your accounts
Read + draft — never send. The AI reads your mail and writes drafts into your Drafts folder. Sending stays a human act: yours, one click per message. Revoke the whole connection any time in settings.
Information you give it

Inbox access — a permission you grant in settings and can revoke there any time — plus one sentence about what counts as urgent in your world. "Anything from a board member, a funder, or about this week's site visits." That sentence is the whole configuration; you already know it by heart.

The recipe
  1. Switch on the Gmail connector — and know what you just granted
    In your AI chat, open Settings → Connectors and connect Gmail. Read the permission screen before you click: the AI will be able to read your mail and create drafts. It cannot send — and even if it could, this recipe never asks it to. You can disconnect in the same settings screen any time, in one click. One honest note: disconnecting stops all future access — it doesn't erase past conversations. Delete any chats you're uneasy about, and check your account's data-retention settings once while you're in there.
  2. Write your one-sentence definition of urgent
    Before the prompt, decide what actually can't wait in your world. "Board members, funders, anything about this week's site visits" — yours will differ. This sentence does more work than anything else in the recipe.
  3. Run the triage
    Paste this, swapping in your time window and your urgent sentence. The draft-only rule is written in — leave it in.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Read my inbox from [Friday 5pm] to now and sort every unread
message into three piles:

A. NEEDS A REPLY FROM ME — a real person is waiting on my answer.
B. HAS A DEADLINE HIDING IN IT — a date, due-by, or RSVP buried
   anywhere in the message. Pull each date out and list these
   soonest-first.
C. FYI — no action needed. One line each.

In my world, urgent means: [anything from a board member or
funder, or anything about this week's site visits]. Put urgent
items at the top of their pile and mark them.

For the routine messages in pile A — scheduling, simple yes/no,
thank-yous — write a reply and save it as a DRAFT ONLY. Never
send anything; I review and send everything myself. If you're
unsure whether a message is routine, don't draft it — just flag
it for me.
  4. Work the piles in order
    Urgent items first, then pile B's deadlines onto your calendar or task list, then the rest of pile A. The 9 a.m. board call just got a lot calmer.
  5. Review every draft, then send each one yourself
    Open your Drafts folder. Read each reply as if a new staff member wrote it — edit freely, delete the misses, and click send on the keepers. Every message that leaves your inbox left because you sent it.
What you walk away with

94 unread turned into three ordered piles and a Drafts folder of ready-to-review replies — before your coffee's cold. And the habit that makes every connected workflow safe: the AI prepares, you approve, you send.

The one hard rule of connected work: nothing goes out without your click. Draft-only, you send. It's written into the prompt — keep it there, and keep it when you adapt this recipe to anything else.
The triage is a smart guess. For the first few weeks, scan the FYI pile yourself before archiving anything — that's where a miss would hide. When you catch one, tighten your "urgent" sentence. Once the piles match your judgment, trust them.
