Connected Workflow · Triage

Monday Morning, Sorted

The AI reads the weekend pileup in your inbox and sorts it: what needs a reply from you, what has a deadline buried in it, and what's just FYI. It drafts replies for the routine ones, and they stay drafts. Nothing leaves without your click.

Connects to your accounts
It reads and drafts, and never sends. The AI reads your mail and writes drafts into your Drafts folder. Sending stays with you, one click per message. You can revoke the whole connection any time in settings.
Information you give it

Inbox access, which is a permission you grant in settings and can revoke there any time, plus one sentence about what counts as urgent in your world. Something like "anything from a board member, a funder, or about this week's site visits." That sentence is the whole configuration, and you already know it by heart.

The recipe
  1. Switch on the Gmail connector and check what you granted
    In your AI chat, open Settings → Connectors and connect Gmail. Read the permission screen before you click. The AI will be able to read your mail and create drafts. It cannot send, and this recipe never asks it to. You can disconnect from the same settings screen any time, in one click. Worth knowing: disconnecting stops all future access but doesn't erase past conversations. Delete any chats you're uneasy about, and check your account's data-retention settings while you're in there.
  2. Write your one-sentence definition of urgent
    Before you paste the prompt, decide what genuinely can't wait in your world. "Board members, funders, anything about this week's site visits" is one example, and yours will differ. This sentence does more work than anything else in the recipe.
  3. Run the triage
    Paste this, swapping in your time window and your urgent sentence. The draft-only rule is written into the prompt, so leave it there.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Read my inbox from [Friday 5pm] to now and sort every unread
message into three piles:

A. NEEDS A REPLY FROM ME — a real person is waiting on my answer.
B. HAS A DEADLINE HIDING IN IT — a date, due-by, or RSVP buried
   anywhere in the message. Pull each date out and list these
   soonest-first.
C. FYI — no action needed. One line each.

In my world, urgent means: [anything from a board member or
funder, or anything about this week's site visits]. Put urgent
items at the top of their pile and mark them.

For the routine messages in pile A — scheduling, simple yes/no,
thank-yous — write a reply and save it as a DRAFT ONLY. Never
send anything; I review and send everything myself. If you're
unsure whether a message is routine, don't draft it — just flag
it for me.
    ROLE:
You are my inbox triage assistant. You sort and draft; you never
send.

GOAL:
Turn a weekend of unread mail into three short piles I can work
through, with the buried deadlines pulled out into daylight.

CONTEXT:
Read my inbox from [Friday 5pm] to now, unread messages only.

In my world, urgent means: [anything from a board member or funder, or
anything about this week's site visits].

STEPS:
1. Sort every unread message into three piles:
   A. NEEDS A REPLY FROM ME — a real person is waiting on my
      answer.
   B. HAS A DEADLINE HIDING IN IT — a date, due-by, or RSVP
      buried anywhere in the message.
   C. FYI — no action needed.
2. For pile B, pull each date out and list the pile
   soonest-first.
3. Put urgent items at the top of their pile and mark them.
4. For the routine messages in pile A — scheduling, simple
   yes/no, thank-yous — write a reply and save it as a DRAFT
   ONLY.
5. If you're unsure whether a message is routine, don't draft
   it — just flag it for me.

OUTPUT:
The three piles, urgent items marked and on top, pile B sorted by
date, pile C one line each. Drafts saved in my drafts folder.
Never send anything; I review and send everything myself.
  4. Work the piles in order
    Take the urgent items first, then move pile B's deadlines onto your calendar or task list, then work the rest of pile A. The 9 a.m. board call gets a lot calmer.
  5. Review every draft, then send each one yourself
    Open your Drafts folder and read each reply as if a new staff member had written it. Edit freely, delete the ones that miss, and click send on the rest. Every message that leaves your inbox leaves because you sent it.
What you walk away with

94 unread messages turned into three ordered piles and a Drafts folder of replies ready to review, before your coffee gets cold. You also pick up the habit that makes every connected workflow safe: the AI prepares, you approve, and you send.

The hard rule of connected work: nothing goes out without your click. The AI drafts and you send. That rule is written into the prompt, so keep it there, and keep it when you adapt this recipe to anything else.
The sorting is a smart guess. For the first few weeks, scan the FYI pile yourself before archiving anything, since that's where a miss would hide. When you catch one, tighten your "urgent" sentence. Once the piles match your judgment, you can rely on them.
