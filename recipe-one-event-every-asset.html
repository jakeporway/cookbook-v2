One event, every asset

You wrote the event description once. Get back the registration blurb, the Instagram caption, the newsletter blip, the reminder email, and the day-of signage, all in your org's actual voice, because you showed it your voice rather than describing it.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

Your new event description, plus one real past example for each channel: an Instagram caption that landed, a reminder email people actually opened. Not a brand guide, and not a paragraph about your tone. The examples are your voice in a way that adjectives never manage.

The recipe
  1. Gather your ingredients (5 minutes of copy-paste)
    Your new event description, plus one past example per channel: a registration blurb, an Instagram caption, a newsletter blip, and a reminder email. Pick ones you were happy with, since everything new will be modeled on them.
  2. Open a normal chat
    No special mode, no project setup. The regular chat window is all this takes.
  3. Paste this prompt with your examples dropped in
    The examples do the heavy lifting. Swap the highlighted parts for your own material.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    I handle communications for [a 6-person community arts nonprofit].
We have a new event coming up. Here's the event description I wrote:

[PASTE YOUR NEW EVENT DESCRIPTION]

Below are real past pieces of ours, one per channel. Match their
voice, length, and format, not a generic nonprofit voice:

REGISTRATION-PAGE BLURB WE LIKED:
[paste it]

INSTAGRAM CAPTION THAT WORKED:
[paste it]

NEWSLETTER BLIP:
[paste it]

REMINDER EMAIL:
[paste it]

Now write one of each for the new event: registration-page blurb,
Instagram caption, newsletter blip, reminder email, and short
day-of signage text (a welcome sign and a directional sign).
Keep every date, time, and location exactly as I wrote them.

Give me your best draft of each. I'll pick and lightly edit,
don't offer endless variations.
    ROLE:
You are our communications writer. You write in our voice,
learned from our own past pieces, not a generic nonprofit voice.

GOAL:
One event description, turned into every asset we need to
promote it, each one already sounding like us.

CONTEXT:
I handle communications for [a 6-person community arts nonprofit]. Here's the event description I
wrote:

[PASTE YOUR NEW EVENT DESCRIPTION]

Below are real past pieces of ours, one per channel. Match their
voice, length, and format:

REGISTRATION-PAGE BLURB WE LIKED:
[paste it]

INSTAGRAM CAPTION THAT WORKED:
[paste it]

NEWSLETTER BLIP:
[paste it]

REMINDER EMAIL:
[paste it]

STEPS:
1. Read the past pieces first and note what's consistent across
   them: length, rhythm, how we open and close.
2. Write one of each for the new event: registration-page blurb,
   Instagram caption, newsletter blip, reminder email, and short
   day-of signage text (a welcome sign and a directional sign).
3. Keep every date, time, and location exactly as I wrote them.
4. Don't add details the event description doesn't contain.

OUTPUT:
The six pieces, labeled by channel. Your best draft of each,
I'll pick and lightly edit, so don't offer endless variations.
  4. Pick and lightly edit, in one pass
    Read each draft, fix the one or two lines that aren't quite you, and move on. If a draft is badly off, the fix is a better example in step 1 rather than more back-and-forth in the chat. See the caution below. Before anything ships, check every date, time, and location against your original. A wrong start time does more damage than a flat sentence ever will.
  5. Post, schedule, and print it yourself
    Copy each piece into your registration platform, scheduler, email tool, and a print document. A person sends everything, which is how the recipe is meant to work.
What you walk away with

The full promotional set for one event: five channels in one voice, in about ten minutes. You also get a habit worth keeping. Store your best examples in one document and every future event starts from paste-and-go rather than a blank page.

If the drafts sound generic, give it a better example rather than a longer description. Swap in a past piece that sounds more like you and run it once more. Stop after that one extra pass. Asking repeatedly for funnier, then more serious, then shorter versions will use up the afternoon this recipe was supposed to save.
