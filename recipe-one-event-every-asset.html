Information you give it

Your new event description, plus one real past example of each channel you liked — an Instagram caption that landed, a reminder email people actually opened. Not a brand guide, not a paragraph about your tone. The examples are your voice; adjectives never will be.

The recipe
  1. Gather your ingredients (5 minutes of copy-paste)
    Your new event description, plus one past example per channel: a registration blurb, an Instagram caption, a newsletter blip, a reminder email. Pick ones you were happy with — they're about to become the mold.
  2. Open a normal Claude chat
    No special mode, no project setup. The regular chat window is all this takes.
  3. Paste this prompt with your examples dropped in
    The examples do the heavy lifting. Swap the highlighted parts for your own material.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    I handle communications for [a 6-person community arts nonprofit].
We have a new event coming up. Here's the event description I wrote:

[PASTE YOUR NEW EVENT DESCRIPTION]

Below are real past pieces of ours, one per channel. Match their
voice, length, and format — not a generic nonprofit voice:

REGISTRATION-PAGE BLURB WE LIKED:
[paste it]

INSTAGRAM CAPTION THAT WORKED:
[paste it]

NEWSLETTER BLIP:
[paste it]

REMINDER EMAIL:
[paste it]

Now write one of each for the new event: registration-page blurb,
Instagram caption, newsletter blip, reminder email, and short
day-of signage text (a welcome sign and a directional sign).
Keep every date, time, and location exactly as I wrote them.

Give me your best draft of each. I'll pick and lightly edit —
don't offer endless variations.
  4. Pick and lightly edit — one pass
    Read each draft, fix the one or two lines that aren't quite you, and move on. If a draft is way off, the fix is in step 1, not in arguing with the chat (see the caution below). Before anything ships, check every date, time, and location digit against your original. A wrong start time does more damage than a flat sentence ever will.
  5. Post, schedule, and print — yourself
    Copy each piece into your registration platform, scheduler, email tool, and a print doc. A human sends everything; that's the design, not a limitation.
What you walk away with

The full promotional set for one event — five channels, one voice, about ten minutes — plus a reusable move: keep your best examples in one doc and every future event starts from "paste and go" instead of a blank page.

If the drafts sound generic, the fix is a better example — not a longer description. Swap in a past piece that sounds more like you and run it once more. And cap iteration at one pass: the "make it funnier… no wait, more serious… shorter?" spiral eats the exact afternoon this recipe was supposed to save. Draft, pick, edit, done.
