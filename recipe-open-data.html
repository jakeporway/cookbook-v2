Pull public data off hard-to-use websites

The county records, license lookups, and funder directories that have no download button. An AI assistant in your browser works through them while you watch and hands you a spreadsheet. For public data only.

Connects to your accounts
Whatever site you send it to. There are no integrations to set up, just the extension's permission for that one site.
Information you give it

The site's URL, the fields you want, and your list of things to look up: org names, addresses, license numbers, or whatever the search box on that site takes.

The recipe
  1. Qualify the source
    This recipe is for public data on sites with no login: public records, government lookups, and open directories. If the data sits behind your own login, use Get your data out of a system with no export button instead. If it sits behind someone else's login or a paywall, stop. This recipe doesn't cover that.
  2. Check whether an export already exists
    Many sites offer a real download or an API if you ask. Checking takes a few minutes and saves much longer, so have the assistant look before you start working through pages one at a time.
  3. Check the site's terms
    The prompt below has the assistant do this with you, first, before it touches anything.
  4. Give it the instruction and your list
    Open the site in the browser extension and give it this. It checks the terms first, then works through your list at a human pace, pausing so you can see its progress.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    We are collecting public data from [SITE]. First, check
whether this site's terms of service, or a bulk-download or API
option, make browsing unnecessary or unwelcome — tell me what you
find before we start.

Then for each item on my list: search it, read the result page,
and record [FIELDS] with the page URL as a source link per row. Leave
any field you cannot find blank — never fill a blank with a guess.
Pause every 15 records and show me the table so far. Go at a human
pace — we are a guest on this site.
    ROLE:
You are collecting public data from someone else's website. You
are a polite guest there: slow, visible, and read-only.

GOAL:
A sourced table of the public information I need — gathered the
least intrusive way available.

CONTEXT:
We are collecting public data from [SITE]. The fields I need are
[FIELDS]. My list of items to look up follows.

STEPS:
1. First, check whether this site's terms of service, or a
   bulk-download or API option, make browsing unnecessary or
   unwelcome. Tell me what you find before we start.
2. Then, for each item on my list: search it, read the result
   page, and record [FIELDS], with the page URL as a source link
   on that row.
3. Leave any field you cannot find blank — never fill a blank
   with a guess.
4. Pause every 15 records and show me the table so far.
5. Go at a human pace. If the site errors, rate-limits, or shows
   a captcha, stop and tell me rather than retrying.

OUTPUT:
First, what you found about bulk downloads, an API, and the terms
of service. Then the table, one row per item with a source link,
shown to me in batches of 15.
  5. Supervise the run from start to finish
    Don't leave this running overnight or unattended. Spot-check 5 rows against the actual pages before you trust the rest. Keep the scale in mind too: dozens of items work in a chat, hundreds work this way, and for thousands you should ask the agency for the dataset, which often exists.
What you walk away with

A spreadsheet the site never offered you, with each row sourced back to the page it came from, built while you watched rather than overnight and unsupervised.

Two separate concerns here: what's permitted, and what's accurate. Public doesn't mean unlimited, and hammering a small county server is rude at best. The human-pace instruction and the step where you ask for the dataset are how to do this considerately. Separately, collected fields follow the same accuracy rule as every lookup recipe: a source link per row, blanks left blank, and a spot-check before you act on anything.
