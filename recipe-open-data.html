Pull public data off hard-to-use websites

The county records, license lookups, and funder directories with no download button: an AI assistant in your browser pages through them for you, while you watch, and hands you a spreadsheet. For public data only.

Connects to your accounts
Whatever site you send it to. No integrations to set up — just the extension's permission for that one site.
Information you give it

The site's URL, the fields you want, and your list of things to look up — org names, addresses, license numbers, whatever the search box on that site takes.

The recipe
  1. Qualify the source
    This recipe is for public data on sites with no login — public records, government lookups, open directories. If the data is behind your own login, that's a different recipe — see Get your data out of a system with no export button. If it's behind someone else's login or a paywall, stop. That's not this recipe.
  2. Check first: does an export already exist?
    Many sites offer a real download or an API if you ask. A quick search for this is always cheaper than browsing — ask the assistant to check before you start paging through anything by hand.
  3. Check the site's terms
    The prompt below has the assistant do this with you, first, before it touches anything.
  4. Give it the instruction and your list
    Open the site in the browser extension, then give it this. It checks terms first, then works your list at a human pace, pausing for you to see its progress.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    We are collecting public data from [SITE]. First, check
whether this site's terms of service, or a bulk-download or API
option, make browsing unnecessary or unwelcome — tell me what you
find before we start.

Then for each item on my list: search it, read the result page,
and record [FIELDS] with the page URL as a source link per row. Leave
any field you cannot find blank — never fill a blank with a guess.
Pause every 15 records and show me the table so far. Go at a human
pace — we are a guest on this site.
  5. Supervise — this runs while you watch
    Not overnight, not unattended. Spot-check 5 rows against the actual pages before you trust the rest. And keep the lookup line in mind: dozens by chat, hundreds this way, thousands means ask the agency for the dataset — it often exists.
What you walk away with

A spreadsheet the site never offered you, each row sourced back to the page it came from, built while you watched instead of overnight and unsupervised.

Two different lines here — legality and accuracy. Public doesn't mean unlimited: hammering a small county server is rude at best. The human-pace instruction and the ask-for-the-dataset step are how good citizens do this. Separately, scraped fields inherit the confabulation rule: source link per row, blanks stay blank, spot-check before you act on anything.
