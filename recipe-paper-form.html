Off Paper · Forms

Rebuild a Paper Form as a Digital One

Photograph your paper intake or registration form; get a working digital version people can fill in, plus a cleaner print version. Only the blank form goes in — never a filled-in copy.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

A photo or scan of the blank form, plus where the responses should end up — email, print, or a spreadsheet. That answer decides which of the two builds below you ask for.

The recipe
  1. Photograph or scan the blank form
    Straight-on, whole form in frame, nothing filled in. Only ever use a blank copy for this recipe.
  2. Decide where responses should land — this decides the build
    For a form people fill out on a shared computer at your front desk, ask for a single HTML file version. For a form people fill out from home, ask it to draft the exact question list and settings to build in Google Forms instead, since Google Forms saves responses properly on its own.
  3. For the front-desk version, paste this prompt

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Rebuild this paper form as a single self-contained HTML file. Same
fields, same order, in plain language labels with large, readable
text.

Include a "Print completed form" button and a "Download responses
as CSV" button.

The file must work completely offline, with no internet connection,
and it must send the data nowhere — everything stays on the
computer it's opened on.

This form is for: [e.g. new-member intake at our front desk]
  4. Test it yourself with fake answers
    Fill it out with made-up information first. Check every field against the original form, and try the print and download buttons.
  5. Have one colleague fill it out before anyone else does
    A second pair of eyes catches a missing field or a confusing label before a real constituent does.
  6. Keep the print version it also drafts
    Someone will always prefer paper, or need it as a backup. Keep both.
What you walk away with

A working digital version of your form — either a self-contained offline file for a shared computer, or a ready-to-build Google Forms plan for people filling it out from home — plus a cleaner print version as backup.

A double-clicked HTML file does not remember anything after it closes. That's a feature for privacy and a trap for collection — if you need responses to accumulate on their own, that's Google Forms territory, and this recipe has the AI draft that build for you instead. Choosing the right side of this line is the actual skill here.
