Rebuild a Paper Form as a Digital One

Photograph your paper intake or registration form and get back a working digital version people can fill in, plus a cleaner print version. Only the blank form goes in, never a filled-in copy.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

A photo or scan of the blank form, plus a decision about where the responses should end up: email, print, or a spreadsheet. That answer decides which of the two builds below you ask for.

The recipe
  1. Photograph or scan the blank form
    Shoot it straight on, with the whole form in frame and nothing filled in. Only ever use a blank copy for this recipe.
  2. Decide where responses should land, which decides the build
    For a form people fill out on a shared computer at your front desk, ask for a single HTML file version. For a form people fill out from home, ask it to draft the exact question list and settings to build in Google Forms instead, since Google Forms saves responses properly on its own.
  3. For the front-desk version, paste this prompt

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Rebuild this paper form as a single self-contained HTML file. Same
fields, same order, in plain language labels with large, readable
text.

Include a "Print completed form" button and a "Download responses
as CSV" button.

The file must work completely offline, with no internet connection,
and it must send the data nowhere, everything stays on the
computer it's opened on.

This form is for: [e.g. new-member intake at our front desk]
    ROLE:
You are building me a small, self-contained tool. I am not a
coder, assume I will open one file and use it.

GOAL:
The attached paper form, rebuilt as a digital one that works on a
front-desk computer with no internet.

CONTEXT:
This form is for: [e.g. new-member intake at our front desk]. The people filling it in may be older,
in a hurry, or reading in a second language, so plain labels and
large type matter more than density.

STEPS:
Rebuild the paper form as a single self-contained HTML file:
1. Same fields, same order as the paper version.
2. Plain-language labels, with large, readable text.
3. Include a "Print completed form" button and a "Download
   responses as CSV" button.
4. It must work completely offline, with no internet connection.
5. It must send the data nowhere, everything stays on the
   computer it's opened on.

OUTPUT:
One HTML file I can double-click, plus a one-line note on where
the downloaded CSV lands and how to back it up.
  4. Test it yourself with fake answers
    Fill it out with made-up information first. Check every field against the original form, and try the print and download buttons.
  5. Have one colleague fill it out before anyone else does
    A second pair of eyes catches a missing field or a confusing label before a real constituent does.
  6. Keep the print version it also drafts
    Someone will always prefer paper, or need it as a backup. Keep both.
What you walk away with

A working digital version of your form, either a self-contained offline file for a shared computer or a ready-to-build Google Forms plan for people filling it out from home, plus a cleaner print version as a backup.

A double-clicked HTML file does not remember anything after it closes. That's good for privacy and a problem for collection. If you need responses to accumulate on their own, use Google Forms, and this recipe will have the AI draft that build for you instead. Choosing correctly between the two is the real skill here.
