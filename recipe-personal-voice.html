Team Systems · Voice

Draft personal notes in your own voice

Fifteen or twenty notes you actually sent — thank-yous, condolences, board nudges — teach it how you write. The endless personal correspondence an ED owes comes out sounding like you on a good day. You still read and send every one.

Sensitive data goes in
Your notes contain other people's names and situations. Pick examples whose content is unremarkable, or lightly blank the identifying details. The STYLE is what's being learned, not the facts.
Information you give it

15–20 real sent notes across the types you write — thank-you, condolence, congratulations, gentle reminder — plus one counter-example: a note that shows how you never want to sound.

The recipe
  1. Collect from your sent folder
    Variety matters more than polish. The awkward-but-warm real ones ARE your voice. Blank out details that feel private — names can become [NAME].
  2. Create a Project named "Notes — my voice"
    In your AI chat: Projects → New project. Add the examples to the Project's knowledge.
  3. Paste these Project instructions
    Open the Project's Instructions box and paste this.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Copy it as-is.

    Project instructions
    These are notes I actually sent. When I ask you to draft one,
write in MY voice as these examples show it — my greeting habits,
my sentence length, my sign-off, the way I reference specifics.
Never use phrases that appear in the counter-example file.

Always leave a [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot where I should add something
only I would know. Drafts are drafts: keep them shorter than feels
complete, because I will add to them — a draft that needs my
addition is working as designed.
  4. Ask for a draft
    Example: "Draft a condolence note to a longtime volunteer whose father died — she organized our coat drive for years."
  5. Add the personal detail by hand, then read it aloud once
    Always fill the [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot yourself before sending. The handwritten sentence is what makes it yours.
  6. Feed it your best ones
    When you send a note you're proud of, drop it into the Project. That's how a living tool gets better.
What you walk away with

A first draft in your own voice, in the time it takes to describe the situation — with a blank spot waiting for the one line only you can write.

The [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot is the integrity of this recipe. A generated note sent untouched is detectable and hollow exactly when it matters most — condolences and gratitude. This tool removes the blank-page delay, not your five minutes of care. For the highest-stakes notes, it's still pen and paper — and you'll know which those are.
