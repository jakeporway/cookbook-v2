Draft personal notes in your own voice

Fifteen or twenty notes you actually sent, covering thank-yous, condolences, and board nudges, teach it how you write. The steady stream of personal correspondence an ED owes comes back sounding like you on a good day. You still read and send every one.

Sensitive data goes in
Your notes contain other people's names and situations. Pick examples whose content is unremarkable, or blank out the identifying details. What's being learned is the style, not the facts.
Information you give it

15–20 real sent notes across the types you write, including thank-you, condolence, congratulations, and gentle reminder, plus one counter-example showing how you never want to sound.

The recipe
  1. Collect from your sent folder
    Variety matters more than polish. The awkward but warm ones are as much your voice as the smooth ones. Blank out details that feel private; names can become [NAME].
  2. Create a Project named "Notes in my voice"
    In your AI chat: Projects → New project. Add the examples to the Project's knowledge.
  3. Paste these Project instructions
    Open the Project's Instructions box and paste this.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Copy it exactly as it is.

    Project instructions 
    These are notes I actually sent. When I ask you to draft one,
write in MY voice as these examples show it: my greeting habits,
my sentence length, my sign-off, the way I reference specifics.
Never use phrases that appear in the counter-example file.

Always leave a [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot where I should add something
only I would know. Drafts are drafts: keep them shorter than feels
complete, because I will add to them, and a draft that needs my
addition is working as designed.
    ROLE:
You draft personal notes in MY voice, as the real examples in
this Project show it, not in a polished professional voice.

GOAL:
A draft I can finish in thirty seconds and send, that still
sounds like it came from me.

CONTEXT:
The notes in this Project are ones I actually sent: my greeting
habits, my sentence length, my sign-off, the way I reference
specifics. There is also a counter-example file: phrases from it
never appear in your drafts.

STEPS:
1. Match my voice from the examples, not a general idea of a warm
   note.
2. Never use phrases that appear in the counter-example file.
3. Always leave a [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot where I should add
   something only I would know.
4. Keep the draft shorter than feels complete. I will add to it.
   A draft that needs my addition is working as designed.

OUTPUT:
One short draft with the [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot left open. No
alternatives unless I ask.
  4. Ask for a draft
    For example: "Draft a condolence note to a longtime volunteer whose father died. She organized our coat drive for years."
  5. Add the personal detail by hand, then read it aloud
    Always fill the [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot yourself before sending. The handwritten sentence is what makes it yours.
  6. Feed it your best ones
    When you send a note you're proud of, add it to the Project. That's how the tool keeps improving.
What you walk away with

A first draft in your own voice, in about the time it takes to describe the situation, with a blank spot waiting for the one line only you can write.

The [PERSONAL DETAIL] slot is what keeps this recipe honest. A generated note sent untouched reads as hollow exactly where it matters most, in condolences and thanks. This tool removes the delay of the blank page, not the five minutes of care you put in. The highest-stakes notes still call for pen and paper, and you'll know which ones those are.
