Off Paper · Photos

Prepare photos safely before you publish them

Before photos of clients, volunteers, or kids go on your website: a small local tool that strips the hidden location and device data from each file and blurs the faces you choose — on your own computer.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

Nothing, at build time. Your photos never enter the chat — that's the point. You only describe what the finished tool should do.

The recipe
  1. Open a normal chat and describe the tool
    No photos needed yet — you're describing what the finished tool should do.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Copy it as-is.

    Prompt
    Build a single self-contained HTML file: I drag photos onto it;
for each photo it (1) removes ALL metadata — GPS location, device
info, timestamps — and shows me what it removed, (2) detects faces
and draws a box on each so I can click which ones to blur (blur
strongly enough that the face is unrecognizable), (3) lets me
download the cleaned copies, originals untouched. Everything runs
locally in the browser — photos must never upload anywhere.
  2. Test it on a phone photo of yourself
    Confirm the GPS data it shows you before cleaning, then confirm the downloaded copy shows none — ask for the tool to include a "verify" view if it doesn't already, so you can check this yourself each time.
  3. Use it for real, per your consent records
    Faces without a signed release get blurred, click by click. Keep your originals in your own files and publish only the cleaned copies.
What you walk away with

Clean copies ready to publish, with the hidden location and device data stripped and every chosen face blurred beyond recognition — and your originals untouched in your own files.

Face detection is good, not perfect. It misses faces at sharp angles and in mirrors and windows. The click-to-choose step means a human looks at every face in every photo before it publishes — that look-over is also when you catch the reflection nobody noticed. And blur tools don't fix a consent problem: no release, no publish, blurred or not.
