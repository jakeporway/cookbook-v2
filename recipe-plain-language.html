Remix Your Content · Simplify

Say it so everyone can read it

Turn your program flyer or intake instructions into a 6th-grade reading level version, a large-print version, and a first-draft Spanish version. The reading level is scored, and a fluent speaker reviews every translation before it goes out.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

The current document, plus the plainest thing your org has ever published: a welcome flyer, a text-message script, or whatever a real person understood on the first read. That piece is the target. Show what "simple and friendly" means rather than describing it.

The recipe
  1. Find your plainest published piece
    Find the clearest thing your org has ever put in front of the public. It doesn't have to relate to this document. It only has to sound the way you wish everything did.
  2. Open a normal chat
    Paste the current document in, or attach the PDF. No special mode is needed.
  3. Paste this prompt with both documents dropped in
    The accuracy rule is the most important part: plain language must never change what people actually have to do.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Here is a document we give to the people we serve. Right now it
reads like the regulation it summarizes:

[PASTE YOUR CURRENT DOCUMENT]

And here is the plainest thing our org has ever published, this
is the register I want, so match how it sounds:

[PASTE YOUR PLAINEST PAST PIECE]

Rewrite the document three ways:

1. PLAIN: 6th-grade reading level or below. Short sentences,
   everyday words, "you" instead of "the applicant". Score it
   (Flesch-Kincaid, a standard readability score; the number is
   roughly the U.S. school grade needed to read it. Aim for 8 or
   below; 6 is great) and tell me the grade level you reached.
2. LARGE-PRINT-FRIENDLY: the plain version restructured for big
   type: short lines, one idea per chunk, clear headings, no
   dense paragraphs.
3. SPANISH FIRST DRAFT: translate the plain version into
   [Spanish]. Mark it "DRAFT, pending human review" at the top.
   Flag any term you weren't sure how to translate.

The hard rule: every requirement, document name, deadline, phone
number, and address stays exactly accurate. If simplifying a
sentence would change what someone has to do, keep it precise and
flag it for me instead.

Give me your best draft of each. I'll pick and lightly edit,
don't offer endless variations.
    ROLE:
You are a plain-language editor for documents that people rely on
to get something they need. Accuracy outranks simplicity every
time.

GOAL:
Three usable versions of this document: plain, large-print
friendly, and a first-draft translation.

CONTEXT:
Here is a document we give to the people we serve. Right now it
reads like the regulation it summarizes:

[PASTE YOUR CURRENT DOCUMENT]

And here is the plainest thing our org has ever published, this
is the register I want, so match how it sounds:

[PASTE YOUR PLAINEST PAST PIECE]

STEPS:
Rewrite the document three ways:
1. PLAIN: 6th-grade reading level or below. Short sentences,
   everyday words, "you" instead of "the applicant." Score it
   (Flesch-Kincaid, a standard readability score; the number is
   roughly the U.S. school grade needed to read it. Aim for 8 or
   below; 6 is great) and tell me the grade level you reached.
2. LARGE-PRINT-FRIENDLY: the plain version restructured for big
   type: short lines, one idea per chunk, clear headings, no
   dense paragraphs.
3. SPANISH FIRST DRAFT: translate the plain version into [Spanish].
   Mark it "DRAFT, pending human review" at the top. Flag any
   term you weren't sure how to translate.

The hard rule throughout: every requirement, document name,
deadline, phone number, and address stays exactly accurate. If
simplifying a sentence would change what someone has to do, keep
it precise and flag it for me instead.

OUTPUT:
The three versions, labeled, with the readability score on the
plain one and a list of anything you kept complicated on purpose.
Your best draft of each, I'll pick and lightly edit, so don't
offer endless variations.
  4. Check the requirements line by line
    Put the plain version next to the original and check that every deadline, document name, and phone number survived. These ten minutes are what make the rest trustworthy.
  5. Send the translation to a fluent speaker
    A bilingual staff member, board member, or trusted volunteer reads the Spanish draft before anyone else does. Their edits are part of the recipe rather than an optional extra. If you have no bilingual staff, a partner org, a stipended community member, or a professional translator reviewing the draft all work. Reviewing a draft costs much less than translating from scratch.
  6. Format and put them into use
    Put each version into your template, using 18pt or larger type for the large-print one, and retire the old letter. Keep all three files together so the next update covers all three.
What you walk away with

Three accessible versions of a document people were struggling with: plain-language with a reading-level score, large-print, and Spanish reviewed by a fluent speaker. You also have a repeatable process for every other confusing document your org hands out.

Machine translation is always a first draft. A fluent speaker reviews any translation before it reaches the people you serve, because a wrong word in intake instructions can cost someone a benefit. Treat the reading-level score as a guide rather than a guarantee. The real test is whether an actual person understands it on the first read.
