Start Here · Setup

Set up your AI the right way

A ten-minute check before anything else: which plan you're on, whether web search is turned on, what your privacy and training settings say, and where Projects live. Half the problems people hit with these recipes come back to setup.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

Nothing. This one is a checklist rather than a prompt, with one exception below.

The recipe

This recipe is a checklist. Step 5 is the one prompt it includes.

  1. Know your plan
    Free plans have tighter limits and fewer features. These recipes assume a paid individual plan on any provider. Check Settings → Plan to see what you're on.
  2. Check the training setting
    In Settings → Privacy, find out whether your conversations can be used to train models, and set it the way your org wants. On a workplace or team plan this is usually already protected. Write down what you found, because every amber recipe in this book depends on that answer.
  3. Turn on web search
    Settings → Features. The "add missing information" recipes in this book need it turned on.
  4. Find Projects in the sidebar
    Categories 4 and 5 of this book are built in Projects. Open one, find the Instructions box and the Add content button, then close it again. You only need to know where they are.
  5. Ask it what it can see
    The AI can't always see your account details. The point of this prompt is to practice asking, and to learn that "check Settings yourself" is sometimes the correct answer.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Copy it exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    What plan am I on, is web search available to me, and can my
conversations here be used for model training with my current
settings? Answer only from what you can actually determine about
this account. For anything you can't determine, say plainly:
"I can't see that from here — check Settings → [location]."
    ROLE:
You are reporting on this account's actual configuration. You are
not describing how the product generally works.

GOAL:
Tell me what I'm actually working with here before I put anything
real into it.

CONTEXT:
I'm about to use this tool for work that may involve other
people's information, so the settings matter more than the
features.

STEPS:
Answer three things, only from what you can actually determine
about this account:
1. What plan am I on?
2. Is web search available to me?
3. Can my conversations here be used for model training with my
   current settings?

For anything you can't determine, do not infer it from the
typical default.

OUTPUT:
A one-line answer to each. Where you can't see something, say
plainly: "I can't see that from here — check Settings →
[location]."
  6. Write down the three answers
    Write down your plan, your training setting, and your web search status on a sticky note, or wherever your team will actually see it. That serves as your org's AI policy until it has a fuller one.
What you walk away with

Three answers you checked rather than assumed: your plan, your training setting, and your search status. Every other recipe in this book assumes you've done this once.

Settings screens move, and plans change names. This checklist describes what to look for rather than exact menu paths. Whichever assistant your org uses, whether that's ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or another, the same four checks exist under different names. Ask the chat itself where its equivalents live.
