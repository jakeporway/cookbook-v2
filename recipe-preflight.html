The recipe

This recipe is a checklist. Step 5 is the one prompt it includes.

  1. Know your plan
    Free plans have tighter limits and fewer features. These recipes assume a paid individual plan (any provider's paid tier). Check Settings → Plan to see what you're on.
  2. Check the training setting
    In Settings → Privacy, find whether your conversations can be used to train models, and set it the way your org wants. On a workplace or team plan, this is typically already protected. Write down what you found — every amber recipe in this book leans on your answer.
  3. Turn on web search
    Settings → Features. The "add missing information" recipes in this book need it turned on.
  4. Find Projects in the sidebar
    Categories 4 and 5 of this book live in Projects. Open one, find the Instructions box and the Add content button, then close it. You just need to know it's there.
  5. Ask it what it can see
    The AI can't always see your account details. The point of this prompt is learning to ask — and learning that "check Settings yourself" is sometimes the right answer.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Copy it as-is.

    Prompt
    What plan am I on, is web search available to me, and can my
conversations here be used for model training with my current
settings? Answer only from what you can actually determine about
this account. For anything you can't determine, say plainly:
"I can't see that from here — check Settings → [location]."
  6. Write down the three answers
    Plan, training setting, web search — on a sticky note, or wherever your team will actually see it. That's your org's one-line AI policy until it has a real one.
What you walk away with

Three answers you actually checked instead of assumed: your plan, your training setting, and your search status. Every other recipe in this book assumes you did this once.

Settings screens move, and plans change names. This checklist names what to look FOR, not pixel-exact paths. Whichever assistant your org uses — ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, or another — the same four checks exist under different names — ask the chat itself where its equivalents live.
