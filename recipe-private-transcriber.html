The Private Transcriber

A page that turns a recorded meeting or interview into a transcript entirely on your own computer. The audio never leaves your machine — usable even for sensitive client conversations.

Where data goes
Nowhere.
It's yours
The audio never leaves your machine. The transcription runs inside your own browser. Claude never hears a recording — it only writes the tool. Nothing sensitive is involved at build time at all.

Information you give it

Nothing sensitive at build time — just the plain facts about the recordings you already have: what format your phone or recorder saves (.m4a, .mp3, .wav), roughly how long they run, and how many voices are on them. The recordings themselves only ever touch your own browser, after the tool exists.

The recipe
  1. Check what your recordings look like
    Find one recording on your phone or recorder and note the file type — usually .m4a or .mp3. That's the only homework.
  2. Open a normal Claude chat and paste this
    Swap in your file type and typical length. No audio is attached — you're describing, not sharing.
    Prompt
    Build me a SINGLE self-contained HTML page that transcribes audio
recordings ENTIRELY in my browser, so the audio is never uploaded
anywhere. Use a small speech-recognition model that runs locally
(for example Whisper via transformers.js). It's OK if the page
downloads the model the first time I open it — after that it should
work with no internet connection.

My recordings are [.m4a files from my phone], usually
[30–60 minutes] long, in [English], with [two speakers].

The page should:
- Let me drag an audio file onto it and show clear progress while
  it works (tell me honestly that long files take a while).
- Show the transcript with timestamps every so often, so I can
  find my place in the audio.
- Give me buttons to copy the transcript and download it as a
  plain text file.
- State plainly on the page that the audio never leaves this
  computer.
  3. Save the file and open it once with internet on
    First open, the page fetches its speech-recognition model — a one-time download, like installing an app. Every use after that is fully offline.
  4. Test it on a recording that isn't sensitive
    Record yourself reading a paragraph, or use a staff-meeting clip. Check the transcript against what was actually said. Local models are good, not perfect — you'll fix the odd word.
  5. Now use it on the real thing
    Drag an intake recording on, let it run while you do something else, and paste the transcript into your case notes. The dreaded typing-up evening is gone.
Private isn't the same as permitted. The audio never leaves your machine — but recording a conversation still requires the other person's consent, every time, per your org's policy and your state's law. This tool changes where the transcript is made; it doesn't change the ethics of making the recording.
What you walk away with

A transcriber that lives on your own computer, costs nothing per use, and can be trusted with your most sensitive conversations — because there is no "somewhere" for the audio to go. Every hour of recording stops costing you an hour of typing.
