Turn a Pile of Receipts Into an Expense Report

Photograph the month's receipts, give it your budget categories, and get back a categorized expense table ready for your bookkeeper, with anything uncertain flagged rather than guessed at.

Sensitive data goes in
Vendor names and amounts go to the AI vendor. That's usually fine for ordinary operating expenses. Skip any receipt that would reveal information about a client, such as a purchase made on their behalf or a service tied to their name.
Information you give it

Receipt photos, plus your actual budget category list, pasted directly from your chart of accounts. Don't describe your categories from memory. Paste the real list so nothing gets invented.

The recipe
  1. Photograph the receipts
    Several per photo is fine, as long as each one lies flat and the printed text is readable. Faded thermal-paper receipts cause the most trouble, so angle them to reduce glare.
  2. Attach the photos in batches
    Send them a manageable batch at a time rather than all at once, so nothing gets skipped.
  3. Paste this prompt, with your real category list

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    For each receipt, extract the date, vendor, and amount, and assign
one category from this list: [PASTE YOUR CATEGORIES].

If the category is not obvious, assign "REVIEW" instead of
guessing. If a total is unreadable, write [UNREADABLE] instead of
guessing.

Output one table sorted by date. After the table, list every REVIEW
and [UNREADABLE] item separately. Then total the amount column so I
can check it against the receipts.
    ROLE:
You are a bookkeeping assistant reading receipts. You transcribe
what's printed; you don't decide what something probably was.

GOAL:
Turn this pile of receipt images into an expense report I can
reconcile.

CONTEXT:
The receipts are attached, photographed, not scanned, so some
will be crooked, faded, or partly cut off. Assign categories from
this list: [PASTE YOUR CATEGORIES].

STEPS:
1. For each receipt, extract the date, the vendor, and the
   amount.
2. Assign exactly one category from my list.
3. If the category is not obvious, assign "REVIEW" instead of
   guessing.
4. If a total is unreadable, write [UNREADABLE] instead of
   guessing.
5. Do not merge or split receipts, even when two look like the
   same purchase.

OUTPUT:
One table sorted by date. After the table, list every REVIEW and
[UNREADABLE] item separately. Then total the amount column so I
can check it against the receipts.
  4. Add up the pile on a calculator once
    Total the actual receipts by hand (or with a calculator app) and compare it to the total the model gave you. The totals must match before any of this goes to the books.
  5. Hand the REVIEW and unreadable items to a person
    Whoever knows the budget best, whether that's you or your bookkeeper, makes the final call on anything the model wouldn't guess at.
What you walk away with

A categorized expense table sorted by date, a separate list of anything uncertain, and a total you've already checked against the physical pile, ready to hand your bookkeeper for the final review.

This is help with data entry, not bookkeeping. Your bookkeeper still reviews every batch. The calculator check isn't optional: a misread digit looks exactly like a real one, and the only way to catch it is to add up the receipts yourself.
