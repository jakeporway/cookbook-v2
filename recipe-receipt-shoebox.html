Turn a Pile of Receipts Into an Expense Report

Photograph the month's receipts, give it your budget categories, and get a categorized expense table ready for your bookkeeper — with anything uncertain flagged, not guessed at.

Information you give it

Receipt photos, plus your actual budget category list — paste it directly from your chart of accounts. Don't describe your categories from memory; paste the real list so nothing gets invented.

The recipe
  1. Photograph the receipts
    Several per photo is fine, as long as each one lies flat and the printed text is readable. Faded thermal-paper receipts are the usual problem spot — angle them to reduce glare.
  2. Attach the photos in batches
    Send them a manageable batch at a time rather than all at once, so nothing gets skipped.
  3. Paste this prompt, with your real category list

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    For each receipt, extract the date, vendor, and amount, and assign
one category from this list: [PASTE YOUR CATEGORIES].

If the category is not obvious, assign "REVIEW" instead of
guessing. If a total is unreadable, write [UNREADABLE] instead of
guessing.

Output one table sorted by date. After the table, list every REVIEW
and [UNREADABLE] item separately. Then total the amount column so I
can check it against the receipts.
  4. Add up the pile on a calculator once
    Total the actual receipts by hand (or with a calculator app) and compare it to the total the model gave you. The totals must match before any of this goes to the books.
  5. Hand the REVIEW and unreadable items to a human
    Whoever knows the budget best — you or your bookkeeper — makes the final call on anything the model wouldn't guess at.
What you walk away with

A categorized expense table sorted by date, a separate list of anything uncertain, and a total you've already checked against the physical pile — ready to hand your bookkeeper for the final review.

This is data entry help, not bookkeeping. Your bookkeeper's review stays in the loop on every batch. The calculator check is not optional — a misread digit looks exactly like a real one, and the only way to catch it is to add up the receipts yourself.

Recipe helper

Helps you sharpen categories that are easy to confuse.

Any categories on your list that tend to get confused with each other?
Supplies vs. program supplies — half our receipts could go either way.
That's common. Give one example receipt for each ambiguous pair directly in the prompt — "a $12 box of pens is Supplies; a $12 box of art materials for a workshop is Program Supplies." Examples beat definitions every time; the model matches your real judgment calls instead of guessing at a rule.
