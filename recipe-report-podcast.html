Remix Your Content · Listen

Your annual report is a podcast

Drop your finished annual report into a free tool and get back a two-host conversation about your year. It's for the supporters who will never read 40 pages but will listen for 12 minutes on a commute.

Public-facing output
Supporters hear this. Listen to every minute before you send it. The AI hosts occasionally overstate things, and an overstated claim in your org's name becomes your problem.
Information you give it

The published annual report PDF, plus an optional one-paragraph note on what to emphasize. That's all. The report supplies everything the two AI hosts talk about.

The recipe
  1. Confirm you're holding the public version
    No draft, no donor appendix, and no internal notes tucked into the PDF. Use the report exactly as it went out the door.
  2. Go to notebooklm.google.com and upload the PDF
    Sign in with any Google account, click New Notebook, then upload the report. NotebookLM is a free Google research tool; its Audio Overview button turns whatever you upload into a two-host audio conversation about it.
  3. Before generating, click customize and paste the focus prompt
    This tells the two hosts what to spend their 10 minutes on, rather than covering the whole document evenly.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Focus on [our three biggest outcomes this year] and the
story of [one program]. Keep every number exactly as it
appears in the report. Warm but factual — no hype, no
superlatives the report doesn't use. Mention that the full
report is on our website.
    ROLE:
You are two hosts talking through our annual report for people
who will never open the PDF.

GOAL:
A conversational audio overview that gets our year across
accurately in the time it takes to drive somewhere.

CONTEXT:
The source is the published annual report, and it is the only
source. Listeners include supporters, partners, and staff — they
will repeat what they hear, so a wrong number travels.

STEPS:
1. Focus on [our three biggest outcomes this year] and the story of [one program].
2. Keep every number exactly as it appears in the report — no
   rounding, no "more than," no combining figures.
3. Stay warm but factual: no hype, and no superlatives the report
   doesn't use itself.
4. Don't add context, comparisons, or sector background that
   isn't in the report.

OUTPUT:
A conversation covering those points, ending with a mention that
the full report is on our website.
  4. Generate the Audio Overview and listen to all of it
    Play it back with the report open in another window. Note anything overstated, out of order, or just wrong.
  5. If a claim is off, regenerate with a steering line
    Add something like "do not discuss X, and keep every number exactly as written," then try once more. Give it one retry, then decide whether to publish it or fall back to text.
  6. Download the audio and put it to work
    Embed it in the year-end email, post it on the site, or send it straight to major donors as a personal touch.
What you walk away with

A shareable audio piece of about 10 minutes, plus the pattern underneath it: the AI writes and performs, and you edit and approve. It's the same draft-and-approve structure as everything else in this book.

The rule for anything front-facing: you are publishing this in your org's voice. Listen to every minute, and if you wouldn't say it to a funder in person, it doesn't go out.
Data note: anything you upload to a large tech company's tool is governed by that tool's terms, which is why this recipe is only for documents that are already public.
