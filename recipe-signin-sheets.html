Type Up Handwritten Sign-In Sheets

Photograph paper sign-in sheets from every session; get a clean attendance spreadsheet in your format. AI chat assistants now read ordinary handwriting from photos well.

Sensitive data goes in
Participant names go to the AI vendor. Check your plan's training settings (covered in the setup recipe) before you start. Consider whether your sign-in sheets are sensitive — for programs serving vulnerable people, this may be a no.
Information you give it

Photos of the sheets, plus one row of your attendance spreadsheet showing your exact column names. It matches your format — you don't reshape anything after.

The recipe
  1. Photograph each sheet flat, in good light
    Lay the sheet flat, avoid glare and shadows, and get the whole page in frame. A blurry photo produces a blurry transcription.
  2. Open a chat and attach up to about 10 photos
    Most chat tools handle a batch of photos in one message. If you have more sheets than that, send them in a second message.
  3. Paste this prompt, with your own column names

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Transcribe every row from these sign-in sheet photos into a table
with columns [YOUR COLUMNS, e.g. Name, Program, Date, Phone].

Mark any name you cannot read confidently as [UNREADABLE] rather
than guessing. Keep rows in the order they appear on each sheet.

After the table, tell me how many entries were marked
[UNREADABLE].
  4. Copy the result into your spreadsheet
    Paste the table in directly — the columns already match what you asked for.
  5. Spot-check 5 random rows against the photos
    Pick five rows anywhere in the table and compare them to the actual photo. This is the step that catches a wrong-but-confident read before it reaches your records.
  6. Save the prompt for next week
    Keep it somewhere you'll find it — a note, a doc, a saved message. Next session, swap in new photos and reuse it as-is.
What you walk away with

A clean attendance table in your own column format, ready to paste into your spreadsheet — plus a flagged list of anything the model couldn't read, so you know exactly where to look.

It will sometimes read a messy name wrong with total confidence. The [UNREADABLE] rule and the 5-row spot-check are the recipe, not extras. Handwriting it genuinely can't read is real — expect a few per sheet, and that's the system working, not failing.
