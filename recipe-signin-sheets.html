Type Up Handwritten Sign-In Sheets

Photograph the paper sign-in sheets from every session and get back a clean attendance spreadsheet in your format. AI chat assistants read ordinary handwriting from photos reasonably well now.

Sensitive data goes in
Participant names go to the AI vendor. Check your plan's training settings, which the setup recipe covers, before you start. Think about whether your sign-in sheets are sensitive. For programs serving vulnerable people, the answer may be that this recipe isn't appropriate.
Information you give it

Photos of the sheets, plus one row of your attendance spreadsheet showing your exact column names. The output matches your format, so there's nothing to reshape afterward.

The recipe
  1. Photograph each sheet flat, in good light
    Lay the sheet flat, avoid glare and shadows, and get the whole page in frame. A blurry photo produces an unreliable transcription.
  2. Open a chat and attach up to about 10 photos
    Most chat tools handle a batch of photos in one message. If you have more sheets than that, send them in a second message.
  3. Paste this prompt, with your own column names

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Transcribe every row from these sign-in sheet photos into a table
with columns [YOUR COLUMNS, e.g. Name, Program, Date, Phone].

Mark any name you cannot read confidently as [UNREADABLE] rather
than guessing. Keep rows in the order they appear on each sheet.

After the table, tell me how many entries were marked
[UNREADABLE].
    ROLE:
You are a transcriber of handwriting. You copy what is on the
page; you never improve it into a plausible name.

GOAL:
Get every row from these photographed sign-in sheets into a table
I can work with.

CONTEXT:
The photos are attached. They're handwritten sheets from real
events — crossed-out lines, cramped columns, and a few people who
signed on the wrong row. The columns I need are [YOUR COLUMNS, e.g. Name, Program, Date, Phone].

STEPS:
1. Transcribe every row from every sheet.
2. Keep rows in the order they appear on each sheet.
3. Mark any name you cannot read confidently as [UNREADABLE]
   rather than guessing. A misread name is worse than a blank —
   it silently becomes a wrong person in our records.
4. Don't normalize spellings, expand nicknames, or fix
   capitalization.

OUTPUT:
One table with columns [YOUR COLUMNS, e.g. Name, Program, Date, Phone]. After the table, tell me how many
entries were marked [UNREADABLE].
  4. Copy the result into your spreadsheet
    Paste the table in directly. The columns already match what you asked for.
  5. Spot-check 5 random rows against the photos
    Pick five rows anywhere in the table and compare them to the actual photo. This is the step that catches a wrong-but-confident read before it reaches your records.
  6. Save the prompt for next week
    Keep it somewhere you'll find it: a note, a doc, or a saved message. Next session, swap in new photos and reuse it unchanged.
What you walk away with

A clean attendance table in your own column format, ready to paste into your spreadsheet, plus a list of anything the model couldn't read so you know exactly where to look.

It will sometimes read a messy name wrong and sound completely sure about it. The [UNREADABLE] rule and the 5-row spot-check are part of the recipe rather than optional extras. Some handwriting genuinely can't be read, so expect a few flags per sheet. That's the system working correctly.
