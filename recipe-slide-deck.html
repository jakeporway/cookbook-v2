Remix Your Content · Present

One story, a real deck

You wrote the quarterly report. The board meets Thursday. Turn the report you already finished into a real PowerPoint file — branded like last year's deck, editable by anyone, ready to email the board chair tonight.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

The finished report or story (the content) plus last year's deck attached as the design template — "make it look like this one." Never a paragraph describing your colors and fonts. The old deck is your brand; adjectives never will be.

The recipe
  1. Pick the source content
    The quarterly report, the program story, the annual letter — whatever's already written and finished. This is the deck's backbone; you're not writing anything new.
  2. Dig up the deck your org already likes
    Last year's board deck, a program-review deck, anything with your colors and layout already baked in. It doesn't need to be recent — it needs to look like "us."
  3. Attach both files and paste this prompt
    The report gives the story; the old deck gives the look. Swap the highlighted parts for your details.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Attached are two files: my written report (the content) and a
slide deck we made before (the design to match).

Build me a PowerPoint deck (a real downloadable .pptx file) that
tells the report's story in [8-10] slides for [our board].

Match the attached deck's colors, fonts, and general layout.

Rules:
- Every number must come from the report — invent nothing.
- One idea per slide. Headlines a board member can read from
  the back of the room.
- End with [what we're asking the board for].
- If a chart would help, build it from the report's numbers and
  note which page they came from in the slide notes.
  4. Open the .pptx and fix what a human should fix
    Open it in PowerPoint or Google Slides. Charts and layout land about 90% right — alignment nitpicks and the last 10% of polish are yours to finish.
  5. Sanity-check every number against the source
    Walk each slide against the original report line by line before it goes anywhere near the board. A wrong figure in front of your board does more damage than a plain slide ever will.
A real file beats a fancy one. An HTML slideshow is a dead end for a board deck — nobody can edit it, forward it, or fix a typo Thursday morning. Always ask for the .pptx.
If the file won't download, or arrives as plain text instead of a file, say exactly this back: "give it to me as a downloadable .pptx file."
What you walk away with

An editable, branded deck ready for Thursday — plus a reusable move: template by example. Next quarter, same two attachments, same prompt, new numbers.
