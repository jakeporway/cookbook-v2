Build a quote library from recorded interviews

Ten-minute recorded conversations with participants become a searchable library of transcribed quotes, tagged by theme and program, that your communications work can draw on all year. Consent is a step in the recipe rather than a footnote.

Sensitive data goes in
Real people's words and stories go into the chat, so handle them accordingly. Transcribe locally first using the private transcriber recipe, and only put quotes covered by consent into the chat.
Information you give it

Transcripts of recorded conversations, made with a signed consent form that names the uses, such as website, reports, and social, before you press record. You'll also need your program names to use as tags.

The recipe
  1. Get consent, then record a 10-minute conversation
    The consent form is signed before recording starts, and it names where quotes may appear. The participant can decline any specific use, such as agreeing to the website but not social, or to internal use only, or to anonymous quotes only. They can also decline the whole thing.
  2. Transcribe on your own machine
    Use the private transcriber recipe so the recording never leaves your computer. Interview audio isn't something to upload to an unfamiliar tool.
  3. Review the transcript and cut anything off the record
    Read it yourself first. Remove anything the person asked to keep off the record, and anything that identifies someone who didn't consent to being named.
  4. Create a Project called "Story bank"
    In your AI chat, go to Projects → New project and name it "Story bank." Add each cleared transcript to the Project knowledge as it becomes ready.
  5. Paste these Project instructions
    Open the Project's Instructions box and paste this. Note what it forbids: it never paraphrases.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Project instructions 
    You hold interview transcripts from people in our programs. When
I ask for quotes, return EXACT verbatim sentences with the
speaker and transcript cited — never paraphrase, never smooth
grammar, never merge sentences from different places in the
transcript.

Tag each quote with a theme and a program from this list:
[YOUR THEME AND PROGRAM LIST]

If nothing in the transcripts fits what I asked for, say so —
never adapt a quote to fit the request. A close paraphrase is
still not a quote.
    ROLE:
You hold interview transcripts from people in our programs. You
are a quote librarian — you retrieve their exact words, you never
improve them.

GOAL:
Find me usable verbatim quotes on a theme, with enough
attribution that I can go back to the source before publishing.

CONTEXT:
The transcripts in this Project are the only source. These are
real people describing real experiences; a smoothed quote is a
words-in-mouth problem, not a style problem.

STEPS:
1. Search the transcripts for material that genuinely fits what I
   asked for.
2. Return EXACT verbatim sentences with the speaker and
   transcript cited.
3. Never paraphrase, never smooth grammar, never merge sentences
   from different places in the transcript.
4. Tag each quote with a theme and a program from this list:
   [YOUR THEME AND PROGRAM LIST]
5. If nothing in the transcripts fits what I asked for, say so.
   Never adapt a quote to fit the request — a close paraphrase is
   still not a quote.

OUTPUT:
Each quote verbatim, with speaker, transcript, theme, and
program. Plus a line telling me what I asked for that the
transcripts don't actually contain.
  6. Ask for quotes, then verify each one
    Try something like "three short quotes about confidence-building from the youth program." Check each quote against the transcript before publishing it anywhere. The citation it gives you makes that quick. Go back for fresh consent when a quote will be used somewhere the original form didn't name.
What you walk away with

A growing, tagged library of real quotes, each one traceable to its transcript and its consent form. Any writer on your team can pull a verified quote in minutes rather than hunting through old recordings.

A paraphrase presented as a quote is a fabricated quote, even when it's close. The never-paraphrase instruction, together with checking against the transcript, is what keeps this useful rather than risky. People's stories belong to them, so consent, the right to decline, and the right to withdraw are permanent parts of the system rather than a step you complete once and forget.
