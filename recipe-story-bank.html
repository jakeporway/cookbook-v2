Build a quote library from recorded interviews

Ten-minute recorded conversations with participants become a searchable library of transcribed quotes, tagged by theme and program, that your communications work draws on all year. Consent is a step in the recipe, not a footnote.

Sensitive data goes in
Real people's words and stories go into the chat — handle them that way. Transcribe locally first with the private transcriber recipe, and only quotes cleared by consent enter the chat.
Information you give it

Transcripts of recorded conversations — record with a signed consent form that names the uses (website, reports, social) before you press record — plus your program names as the tagging vocabulary.

The recipe
  1. Record a 10-minute conversation — consent first
    The consent form is signed before recording starts, and it names where quotes may appear. The participant can decline any specific use (website, but not social; internal only; anonymous only) and can say no to the whole thing.
  2. Transcribe on your own machine
    Use the private transcriber recipe so the recording never leaves your computer. Interview audio is not something to upload to an unfamiliar tool.
  3. Review the transcript and cut anything off the record
    Read it yourself first. Remove anything the person asked to keep off the record, and anything that identifies someone who didn't consent to being named.
  4. Create a Project called "Story bank"
    In your AI chat: Projects → New project → "Story bank." Add each cleared transcript to Project knowledge as it's ready.
  5. Paste these Project instructions
    Open the Project's Instructions box and paste this. Note what it forbids: it never paraphrases, ever.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Project instructions
    You hold interview transcripts from people in our programs. When
I ask for quotes, return EXACT verbatim sentences with the
speaker and transcript cited — never paraphrase, never smooth
grammar, never merge sentences from different places in the
transcript.

Tag each quote with a theme and a program from this list:
[YOUR THEME AND PROGRAM LIST]

If nothing in the transcripts fits what I asked for, say so —
never adapt a quote to fit the request. A close paraphrase is
still not a quote.
  6. Ask for quotes, then verify each one
    Try: "three short quotes about confidence-building from the youth program." Check each quote against the transcript before publishing anywhere — the citation it gives you makes this fast, not slow. Refresh consent when a quote is used somewhere new from what the original form named.
What you walk away with

A growing, tagged library of real quotes, each traceable to its transcript and its consent form. Any writer on your team can pull a verified quote in minutes instead of hunting through old recordings.

A paraphrase presented as a quote is a fabricated quote, even when it's close. The never-paraphrase instruction, plus checking against the transcript, is what makes this a library and not a liability. People's stories are theirs: consent, the right to decline, and the right to withdraw are permanent parts of the system — not a step you do once and forget.
