Information you give it

One column from the survey export you already have: the answers to the open-ended question, copied straight out of the spreadsheet. If any answers name people — staff, participants, sites — run the column through the redaction pass (Recipe 01) first.

The recipe
  1. Copy just the response column
    Open the survey export, click the column header for the open-ended question, copy. Leave the names, emails, and demographics columns behind — the themes don't need them.
  2. Skim for names — and redact if you find any
    "Ms. Rivera was amazing" and "the guy at the Dorchester site" are both identifying. If answers mention people or places like that, run the column through the redaction pass before pasting. Takes two minutes and makes every step after this one safe.
  3. Paste this prompt with the responses inside it
    Swap in your actual survey question and your responses. If the chat says your message is too long, split the responses into two batches of ~150 and ask the same question twice — then ask it to merge the two theme lists.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Below are all the responses to one open-ended question from our
post-program survey. The question was:
"What could we improve about the program?"

Read every response and give me:
1. The 4–6 themes that genuinely recur, named in plain language a
   board member would understand — no jargon.
2. How many responses touch each theme (one response can count
   toward more than one), roughly ranked by frequency.
3. Three VERBATIM quotes per theme — copied word for word, typos
   and all. Do not paraphrase, trim, or clean up grammar.
4. A short "didn't fit any theme" list, so nothing disappears
   silently.

Don't invent a theme to reach a rounder number. If something only
comes up two or three times but seems serious, call it out
separately rather than promoting it to a theme.

RESPONSES:
[paste the response column here]
  4. Spot-check the quotes against the source
    Search your spreadsheet (Ctrl+F / Cmd+F) for a distinctive phrase from each quote and confirm it's really there, word for word. AI can occasionally blend two answers into a quote nobody actually wrote — this two-minute check is what makes the quotes safe to use.
  5. Dig into the theme that matters most
    Ask a follow-up in the same chat: "Show me every response that touched the scheduling theme." Now you're reading the 40 answers that matter instead of all 300.
  6. Save the prompt for the next survey cycle
    Two years of unread feedback took one afternoon. The next batch takes ten minutes.
What you walk away with

A plain-language theme summary with counts, verified verbatim quotes ready for the board deck or funder report, and an "other" pile that proves nothing got swept under the rug. Feedback that finally got read — all of it.

Two rules before this leaves your desk. First: if answers name people, run the column through the redaction pass (Recipe 01) before pasting — survey respondents didn't sign up to be pasted into a chat. Second: spot-check every verbatim quote against the source spreadsheet before quoting anyone to a funder. A blended or misremembered quote in a funder report costs trust you can't buy back.
