The volunteer training kit

The training doc you already have becomes a full kit — a facilitator guide, a plain-language handout, and a click-through practice quiz that runs on any laptop or phone, no login required.

Information you give it

The training document you already use, plus the questions new volunteers actually ask — from memory or your inbox. Five is plenty. Real questions beat a description of what people find confusing.

The recipe
  1. Gather the doc and jot the real questions
    Pull up your current training document, then list the 5 or so questions new volunteers actually ask you — not the ones you wish they'd ask.
  2. Paste the prompt and attach the document
    One prompt, three outputs: a facilitator guide, a plain-language handout, and a self-contained practice quiz.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    Attached is our volunteer training document. Build me three
things from it:

1. A one-page FACILITATOR GUIDE — the session in 6 steps, with
   timing, and the [5] questions new volunteers actually ask
   (listed below) answered plainly.
2. A PLAIN-LANGUAGE HANDOUT volunteers keep — 6th-grade reading
   level (a readability score of 6 — roughly the school grade
   needed to read it), the safety rules impossible to miss.
3. A PRACTICE QUIZ as a single self-contained HTML file that runs
   in a browser with no internet — [10] questions, instant
   feedback with a one-line explanation quoting the handout, a
   score at the end, nothing recorded or sent anywhere.

Questions the volunteers actually ask:
[list them]

Every fact must come from the attached document — invent
nothing; if the document doesn't cover one of the questions,
say so instead of answering.
  3. Review it like a trainer, not a proofreader
    Is anything wrong, out of date, or unsafe to leave ambiguous? Fix the content in YOUR document first — it stays the source of truth — then regenerate from the corrected doc.
  4. Save the quiz file and take it yourself
    Save the HTML file, double-click it, and click through it once as if you were a brand-new volunteer, before anyone else sees it.
  5. Run one cohort, then feed back what confused people
    After the first training, note where volunteers stumbled and regenerate that section. This is the living-tool loop — the kit gets sharper cohort over cohort.
What you walk away with

A training kit that gets sharper each cohort, plus the pattern underneath it: one source document, many teaching formats — a facilitator guide, a handout, and a quiz, all traceable back to the same doc.

The browser line, taught here: the quiz gives volunteers practice — it does NOT track completion. The moment you need "who passed, on record," that's an LMS (learning management system — training software with logins), not a clickable file. Ask the chat what a good next step would be when you get there.
The doc is the boss: if the AI's guide says something your document doesn't, the fix goes in your document first, then regenerate — never let the kit drift from the source.

What does your training cover, and is any of it hands-on?
Our training has a hands-on food-safety part you can't learn from a screen.
Keep that hands-on — don't try to quiz your way around it. Tell the AI: "the quiz covers policies only; for food handling, every question should end with 'you'll practice this in person.'" The kit prepares people so the in-person hour is spent on hands, not paperwork.
