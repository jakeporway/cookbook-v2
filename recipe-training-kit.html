Remix Your Content · Teach

The volunteer training kit

The training document you already have becomes a full kit: a facilitator guide, a plain-language handout, and a click-through practice quiz that runs on any laptop or phone with no login required.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

The training document you already use, plus the questions new volunteers actually ask, taken from memory or your inbox. Five is plenty. Real questions work better than a description of what people find confusing.

The recipe
  1. Gather the doc and jot the real questions
    Pull up your current training document, then list the five or so questions new volunteers actually ask you, rather than the ones you wish they'd ask.
  2. Paste the prompt and attach the document
    One prompt produces three things: a facilitator guide, a plain-language handout, and a self-contained practice quiz.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Attached is our volunteer training document. Build me three
things from it:

1. A one-page FACILITATOR GUIDE: the session in 6 steps, with
   timing, and the [5] questions new volunteers actually ask
   (listed below) answered plainly.
2. A PLAIN-LANGUAGE HANDOUT volunteers keep: 6th-grade reading
   level (a readability score of 6, roughly the school grade
   needed to read it), the safety rules impossible to miss.
3. A PRACTICE QUIZ as a single self-contained HTML file that runs
   in a browser with no internet: [10] questions, instant
   feedback with a one-line explanation quoting the handout, a
   score at the end, nothing recorded or sent anywhere.

Questions the volunteers actually ask:
[list them]

Every fact must come from the attached document, invent
nothing; if the document doesn't cover one of the questions,
say so instead of answering.
    ROLE:
You are a volunteer trainer building a kit from our existing
training document. The document is your only source of fact.

GOAL:
Three usable pieces from one document: something to run the
session from, something volunteers keep, and something that
checks they got it.

CONTEXT:
Our volunteer training document is attached. Here are the
questions the volunteers actually ask, which the kit needs to
answer:
[list them]

STEPS:
Build three things from the document:
1. A one-page FACILITATOR GUIDE: the session in 6 steps, with
   timing, and the [5] questions above answered plainly.
2. A PLAIN-LANGUAGE HANDOUT volunteers keep: 6th-grade reading
   level (a readability score of 6, roughly the school grade
   needed to read it), with the safety rules impossible to miss.
3. A PRACTICE QUIZ as a single self-contained HTML file that runs
   in a browser with no internet: [10] questions, instant
   feedback with a one-line explanation quoting the handout, a
   score at the end, nothing recorded or sent anywhere.

Every fact must come from the attached document, invent nothing.
If the document doesn't cover one of the questions, say so
instead of answering.

OUTPUT:
The three pieces, plus a short list of the volunteer questions
the document doesn't answer, those are for a human to fill in.
  3. Review it like a trainer, not a proofreader
    Look for anything wrong, out of date, or too ambiguous to be safe. Fix the content in YOUR document first, since it stays the source of truth, then regenerate from the corrected version.
  4. Save the quiz file and take it yourself
    Save the HTML file, double-click it, and click through it once as if you were a brand-new volunteer, before anyone else sees it.
  5. Run one cohort, then feed back what confused people
    After the first training, note where volunteers stumbled and regenerate that section. The kit gets better with each cohort this way.
What you walk away with

A training kit that improves with each cohort, plus the pattern underneath it: one source document turned into several teaching formats, with the facilitator guide, the handout, and the quiz all traceable back to the same file.

Here's where the browser line falls: the quiz gives volunteers practice, but it does NOT track completion. As soon as you need a record of who passed, you need a learning management system, meaning training software with logins, rather than a clickable file. Ask the chat about good options when you reach that point.
The document is the authority. If the AI's guide says something your document doesn't, fix the document first and then regenerate. Don't let the kit drift away from its source.
