Turn Whiteboard Photos Into a Real Document

Photograph the flip-charts and whiteboards from your retreat and hand them over, and they come back as a structured document covering themes, decisions, owners, and the parking lot, while people still remember what the arrows meant.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

Photos of each board, plus one line of context per board, such as "this one is from the fundraising breakout." That single line does more work than you might expect.

The recipe
  1. Photograph every board before anyone erases it
    Shoot straight on with the whole board in frame. This habit is what makes the rest of the recipe possible, since nobody can reconstruct a half-erased board, AI or otherwise.
  2. Attach the photos with a one-line label for each
    Label them "Board 1, fundraising breakout," "Board 2, full group wrap-up," and so on.
  3. Paste this prompt

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    Transcribe everything on these whiteboard photos. Keep each board
separate, using the labels I've given them: [Board 1: fundraising
breakout, Board 2: full group wrap-up, ...]. Preserve lists and
groupings as they appear.

Where arrows or circles connect items, describe the connection in
words rather than just noting that a line exists.

Then organize everything into:
1. Themes discussed
2. Decisions made
3. Action items, with an owner if one is written down
4. Open questions (parking lot)

Mark anything you cannot read as [UNREADABLE], and anything you
inferred, like what an arrow or circle means, as [INFERRED], so I
can check it.
    ROLE:
You are transcribing whiteboards from a working session. You
report what's on the board and label anything you interpreted.

GOAL:
Turn photos of our whiteboards into a document the people who
weren't in the room can actually use.

CONTEXT:
The photos are attached. The boards are labeled: [Board 1: fundraising breakout, Board 2: full group
wrap-up, ...]. Keep
each board separate. They were different conversations.

STEPS:
1. Transcribe everything on each board, preserving lists and
   groupings as they appear.
2. Where arrows or circles connect items, describe the connection
   in words rather than just noting that a line exists.
3. Then organize everything across the boards into:
   1. Themes discussed
   2. Decisions made
   3. Action items, with an owner if one is written down
   4. Open questions (parking lot)
4. Mark anything you cannot read as [UNREADABLE], and anything
   you inferred, like what an arrow or circle means, as
   [INFERRED], so I can check it.

OUTPUT:
The per-board transcription first, then the four organized
sections, with every [UNREADABLE] and [INFERRED] tag left in
place.
  4. Review it with one other person who was in the room
    Two people who were actually in the room will catch a wrong [INFERRED] connection much faster than one person working alone.
  5. Share the document while memories are fresh
    Send it to the team the same day if you can. A week later, people find the arrows just as hard to interpret as the model did.
What you walk away with

A structured retreat document covering themes, decisions, owners, and open questions, checked by someone who was in the room and sent out while people still remember what the arrows meant.

The transcription is reliable, but the interpretation of arrows and circles is a labeled guess. That's why [INFERRED] items need checking by someone who was there. And photograph before erasing, since that's the one habit the whole recipe depends on.
