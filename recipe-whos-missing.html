Who's Missing? (The Three-List Reconcile)

Who registered, who attended, and who completed the survey, and where the gaps are between them. Paste the three lists and get the answers back as plain lists of names rather than a pivot table.

Nothing to flag. No sensitive data goes in, and nothing gets published
Information you give it

The three lists you already have: the registration export, the sign-in sheet or attendance export, and the survey-completion list. If the data is personal enough that you wouldn't email it around, use just their column headers with the local-tool version below.

The recipe
  1. Pick your privacy tier for this data, this time
    A quick test: if the lists contain Social Security numbers, health information, anything about minors, or home addresses, use the on-your-computer version below. If they hold only names and work emails, pasting is fine. In that case continue to step 2. Otherwise, skip to the local-tool version in the caution box below.
  2. Export or copy the three lists
    A name and email per person is plenty. The formats don't need to match: one can be a CSV, one a sign-in sheet you typed up, and one a survey export. The AI sorts that out.
  3. Paste this prompt with the three lists inside it
    Swap in your own lists and, if you like, your own program name.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Prompt 
    I coordinate a workforce training program and I'm reconciling three
lists before a quarterly review.

LIST A: people who REGISTERED:
[paste the registration list here]

LIST B: people who ATTENDED:
[paste the attendance list here]

LIST C: people who COMPLETED THE SURVEY:
[paste the survey-completion list here]

Match people across the lists by email address. Where an email is
missing, match by name, but FLAG those matches as uncertain rather
than assuming. Names may be spelled slightly differently between lists.

Give me plain lists of names (please, no pivot tables):
1. Registered but never attended
2. Attended but never registered (walk-ins)
3. Attended but didn't complete the survey
4. Made it all the way through all three
5. Uncertain matches you had to guess about, shown side by side

End with a count for each group, and tell me if any list looks like
it has duplicate rows inside itself.
    ROLE:
You are reconciling three lists of people. Where you can't be
certain two rows are the same person, you say so rather than
deciding.

GOAL:
Know who fell out of the funnel between registering, attending,
and finishing the survey, before a quarterly review.

CONTEXT:
I coordinate a workforce training program and I'm reconciling three lists.

LIST A: people who REGISTERED:
[paste the registration list here]

LIST B: people who ATTENDED:
[paste the attendance list here]

LIST C: people who COMPLETED THE SURVEY:
[paste the survey-completion list here]

STEPS:
1. Match people across the lists by email address.
2. Where an email is missing, match by name, but FLAG those
   matches as uncertain rather than assuming. Names may be
   spelled slightly differently between lists.
3. Sort everyone into the five groups below.
4. Check each list for duplicate rows inside itself.
5. Give me plain lists of names, please, no pivot tables.

OUTPUT:
1. Registered but never attended
2. Attended but never registered (walk-ins)
3. Attended but didn't complete the survey
4. Made it all the way through all three
5. Uncertain matches you had to guess about, shown side by side

End with a count for each group, and tell me if any list looks
like it has duplicate rows inside itself.
  4. Read the gaps, and check the uncertain-match pile
    Group 5 is where mistakes hide. "Mike Chen" on the sign-in sheet and "Michael Chen" in the survey might be the same person. You know your participants and the AI doesn't, so resolve those few yourself.
  5. Spot-check three names before the numbers go anywhere
    Pick three people from the "attended but no survey" list and confirm them against the raw exports. Two minutes of checking means you can stand behind the completion numbers in the review.
  6. Save the prompt for next quarter
    The lists change but the prompt doesn't. Keep it wherever you keep your quarterly checklist.
What you walk away with

Named lists showing exactly where each person dropped off: who to nudge about the survey, who registered and never appeared, plus clean counts for the quarterly review. It's follow-up you can act on today rather than a pivot table you'd still have to decode.

Choose your privacy tier for THIS data, this time. If these are lists you'd email a colleague, such as staff trainings or a public workshop, paste them freely. If they hold people's personal information, such as clients, minors, or home contacts, use the green version instead: paste only the column headers of the three lists and use the prompt below. You get the same answer, and the names never leave your machine.

You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

Prompt (green version, headers only) 
I will NOT paste the data, here are just the column headers of my
three lists:

LIST A: REGISTERED: [Name, Email, Registration Date]
LIST B: ATTENDED: [Name, Email, Check-in Time]
LIST C: COMPLETED THE SURVEY: [Name, Email, Submitted Date]

Build me a SINGLE self-contained HTML file (no internet needed, no
libraries loaded from the web) where I can drop the three CSVs onto
the page. It must read the files in my browser only, my data should
never be uploaded anywhere, and show me the same five groups as
before:
1. Registered but never attended
2. Attended but never registered (walk-ins)
3. Attended but didn't complete the survey
4. Made it all the way through all three
5. Uncertain matches you had to guess about, shown side by side

End with a count for each group.
ROLE:
You are building me a small, self-contained tool. I am not a
coder, assume I will open one file and use it.

GOAL:
The same three-list reconcile as above, but as a page I keep, so
the participant data never leaves my computer.

CONTEXT:
I will NOT paste the data, here are just the column headers of
my three lists:

LIST A: REGISTERED: [Name, Email, Registration Date]

Build a SINGLE self-contained HTML file (no internet needed, no
libraries loaded from the web) where I drop the three CSVs onto
the page. It must read the files in my browser only, my data
should never be uploaded anywhere. It should:

   slightly different spellings, and show those matches as
