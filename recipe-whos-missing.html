Who's Missing? (The Three-List Reconcile)

Registered vs. attended vs. completed-the-survey — who fell through which crack? Paste the three lists and get the gaps as plain lists of names, not a pivot-table lecture.

Nothing to flag — no sensitive data in, nothing published out
Information you give it

The three lists you already have: the registration export, the sign-in sheet or attendance export, and the survey-completion list. Or — if this data is personal enough that you wouldn't email it around — just their column headers, for the local-tool variant below.

The recipe
  1. First, pick your privacy tier — for this data, this time
    A quick test: if the lists contain Social Security numbers, health information, anything about minors, or home addresses — use the on-your-computer variant below. If it's just names and work emails, pasting is fine. If that's you, continue to step 2. If not, skip to the local-tool variant in the caution box below instead.
  2. Export or copy the three lists
    Name and email per person is plenty. They don't need to match in format — one can be a CSV, one a sign-in sheet you typed up, one a survey export. The AI sorts out the mess.
  3. Paste this prompt with the three lists inside it
    Swap in your own lists and, if you like, your own program name.

    You don't need to understand every word below — it's instructions for the AI, not for you. Swap the [bracketed] parts; copy the rest exactly as-is.

    Prompt
    I coordinate a workforce training program and I'm reconciling three
lists before a quarterly review.

LIST A — people who REGISTERED:
[paste the registration list here]

LIST B — people who ATTENDED:
[paste the attendance list here]

LIST C — people who COMPLETED THE SURVEY:
[paste the survey-completion list here]

Match people across the lists by email address. Where an email is
missing, match by name — but FLAG those matches as uncertain rather
than assuming. Names may be spelled slightly differently between lists.

Give me plain lists of names (please, no pivot tables):
1. Registered but never attended
2. Attended but never registered (walk-ins)
3. Attended but didn't complete the survey
4. Made it all the way through all three
5. Uncertain matches you had to guess about, shown side by side

End with a count for each group, and tell me if any list looks like
it has duplicate rows inside itself.
  4. Read the gaps — and check the uncertain-match pile
    Group 5 is where mistakes hide. "Mike Chen" on the sign-in sheet and "Michael Chen" in the survey might be one person. You know your participants; the AI doesn't. Resolve those few by eye.
  5. Spot-check three names before the numbers go anywhere
    Pick three people from the "attended but no survey" list and confirm them against the raw exports. Two minutes, and now you can stand behind the completion numbers in the review.
  6. Save the prompt for next quarter
    The lists change; the prompt doesn't. Keep it wherever you keep your quarterly checklist.
What you walk away with

Named lists of exactly who fell through which crack — who to nudge about the survey, who registered and vanished — plus clean counts for the quarterly review. Follow-up you can act on today, instead of a pivot table you'd still have to decode.

Choose your privacy tier for THIS data, this time. If these are lists you'd email a colleague — staff trainings, a public workshop — paste freely. If they hold people's personal information — clients, minors, home contacts — use the green variant instead: paste only the column headers of the three lists and use the prompt below. Same answer; the names never leave your machine.

Prompt (green variant — headers only)
I will NOT paste the data — here are just the column headers of my
three lists:

LIST A — REGISTERED: [Name, Email, Registration Date]
LIST B — ATTENDED: [Name, Email, Check-in Time]
LIST C — COMPLETED THE SURVEY: [Name, Email, Submitted Date]

Build me a SINGLE self-contained HTML file (no internet needed, no
libraries loaded from the web) where I can drop the three CSVs onto
the page. It must read the files in my browser only — my data should
never be uploaded anywhere — and show me the same five groups as
before:
1. Registered but never attended
2. Attended but never registered (walk-ins)
3. Attended but didn't complete the survey
4. Made it all the way through all three
5. Uncertain matches you had to guess about, shown side by side

End with a count for each group.
