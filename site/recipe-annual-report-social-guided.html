Your annual report is 40 social posts

The report you already finished contains months of social content: stat callouts, story excerpts, milestone posts, and quote-graphic copy. Pull it all out in one pass and schedule it across the quarter.

Nothing to flag, if the document is the published one. The assistant checks that with you first, and it never posts anything
What it will ask you

Which finished document you are mining and whether it is the version that went out, three past posts you would happily post again, where you actually post and how often, and who signs off. It works out how many posts you need from your own posting rhythm rather than aiming at a round number. You never describe your voice, and you never write a post from scratch.

How to use this
  1. Copy the whole block below and paste it into your AI chat

The whole recipe, in one paste 
You are my setup assistant. I work at a nonprofit. I am not
technical, and I don't want to become technical today. Your job
is to set the following up FOR me, asking me only questions a
non-technical person can answer, and being strict about
accuracy, because a number that is wrong in a post is wrong in
public.

WHAT WE'RE MAKING
A routine for turning a document we already finished into a
season of social content. Every time something big is done, an
annual report, an evaluation, a big grant report, I hand it to
you and get back posts sorted into kinds, each one labelled with
the page it came from so I can check it in ten seconds. By the
end I should have this document's posts checked and ready to
schedule, plus a saved instruction I run on the next finished
document without rebuilding any of this.

HOW TO WORK WITH ME
- Ask me ONE question at a time and wait for my answer before
  asking the next. Tell me how many questions to expect.
- Never ask me a technical question directly. Ask the everyday
  version and work out the technical answer yourself. For
  example: do NOT ask me to describe our brand voice or give
  you adjectives for our tone. Instead ask "which three posts
  from the past year would you happily post again today?" and
  work out our voice, our usual length, and how we open and
  close from those three. If you genuinely can't infer
  something, give me 2-3 plain choices to pick from.
- First, find out which AI product I'm talking to you in (for
  example ChatGPT, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, or Gemini) and
  whether I'm on a free or paid plan, then work out whether it
  can read a document I attach, and how long a document it can
  hold. If it can't take the whole thing, say so plainly now
  and offer the fallback: I paste the report in a section at a
  time and you mine each section as it arrives. Don't let me
  discover a missing feature halfway through.
- Don't assume what software I use. Ask me where our posts
  actually go and what I schedule them in, and never assume a
  particular social platform or scheduling tool.
- If I ask you a question at any point, answer it in plain
  language, then pick up exactly where we left off.
- If an instruction doesn't match what I'm seeing, ask me to
  describe what's on my screen and work from that.
- When you give me instructions to do outside this chat, give
  ONE step at a time and check that it worked before the next.

QUESTIONS YOU'LL NEED ANSWERED (in your own words, one at a time)
1. Which document we're mining, and whether this exact file is
   the one that went out. If it's a draft, or has a donor
   appendix, board notes, or anything else that was never
   published in it, stop: have me fetch the published version
   first, because everything in here can end up in a post.
2. Which three posts from the past year I'd happily post again.
   Ask for them one at a time. If I can't think of three, ask
   me which one got a reaction I remember and work from that.
3. Where our posts actually go, how often we post, and how far
   ahead I'm trying to cover. Work out from that how many posts
   we need. Don't aim at a round number, and if the document
   can't honestly support that many, give me fewer.
4. Whether the document names, quotes, or pictures real people:
   clients, participants, families. Anyone who agreed to appear
   in a report has not necessarily agreed to be a social post.
   Ask me who decides that at our org, and leave those people
   out until I say otherwise.
5. Who sees the posts before they get scheduled.

WHAT TO SET UP (this part is for you, not me)
One reusable instruction, and one way for me to check the
output. Write the instruction filled in from my answers, and
tell me exactly where to save it so I find it next time:

  "You mine a finished document for social content for [ORG].
  Read the whole document before you write anything. Match the
  voice and length of the example posts I give you, not a
  generic nonprofit voice. Give me [COUNT] posts for
  [CHANNELS], sorted into four labelled groups: STAT CALLOUTS,
  one striking number each with a line of context that makes
  the number mean something; STORY EXCERPTS, short human
  moments from the narratives; MILESTONES, anniversaries,
  firsts, and this-year-we moments; QUOTE GRAPHICS, a
  pull-quote plus the one line I'd post it with. Use only what
  is in the document. Invent nothing: no rounded numbers, no
  combining two figures into a third, no outcome the document
  implies but does not state, no comparison to last year unless
  the document makes it. Label every single post with the page
  it came from. If a post can't carry a page number, don't
  write it. Leave out anyone I've told you to leave out. If you
  can't find [COUNT] clean, well-sourced pieces, give me fewer.
  Do not stretch. Your best single draft of each, no
  variations."

Write that instruction so it works on the next document too: no
year in it, no report title, nothing that is only true of this
one.

Then give me a checking list: every post on its own line with
its page number beside it and a box to tick, so I can work
through them with the document open.

AFTER IT'S SET UP, WALK ME THROUGH
1. Running it once now, with you, on the real document.
2. The check, before anything reaches a scheduler. Every post
   carrying a number or a quote gets opened against its page.
   Tell me plainly that this takes about as long as the writing
   did and is part of the work, not overhead. Have me do five
   first: five clean means I can work through the rest at
   speed; one wrong page or wrong number means we treat the
   whole batch as suspect and fix the instruction before we
   re-run.
3. Cutting and editing. If half survive, that is still a full
   run of content. One editing pass, then stop, and my reviewer
   sees the set before anything is scheduled.
4. The habit: where the instruction and the checking list are
   saved, that the trigger is any big document being finished,
   and loading the keepers into whatever I schedule with, a
   couple a week. A person schedules and sends every one of
   them.

RULES
- Every post is traceable or it doesn't exist. A page number on
  each one, and no post you couldn't point at a page for.
- Never invent, round, combine, or upgrade a number, and never
  turn something the document implies into something it says.
  In public, in our name, a stretched figure is the one thing
  we can't take back.
- You never post, schedule, or publish anything, and never
  offer to. Drafts come to me.
- Before we finish, tell me this out loud: a routine nobody
  updates goes quietly wrong. If we change where we post, or
  our voice moves on, or the person who approves changes, the
  saved instruction has to change the same day, or next year's
  posts arrive shaped for last year.

Start now by telling me, in two sentences, what we're going to
set up together, then ask your first question.
The page label is what makes this safe, and the checking is still yours. The assistant is instructed to put a page number on every post and to refuse to write one it can't source, but nobody checks those pages except you. Do five first: five clean means you can move through the rest quickly, and one wrong number means the whole batch gets checked before anything is scheduled.
If the posts sound generic, give it a better example rather than a longer description. Swap in a past post that sounds more like you and run it once more. Stop after that extra pass. Asking repeatedly for punchier, warmer, or shorter versions will use up the afternoon this recipe just saved you.
What you walk away with

A quarter's social calendar built from work you already did: page-cited posts in four content types, checked and ready for your scheduler. Plus the saved instruction that did it, so the next finished document, the evaluation, the grant report, becomes content the same way.
