Explain your finances in board-ready language

Your budget-versus-actuals export becomes a plain-language summary a board member can read in five minutes: where the money went, what's off track and by how much, and the questions worth asking. It handles the translation and you verify the numbers.

Sensitive data goes in
Internal, but usually not personal. Budget-vs-actuals numbers describe the organization rather than individuals. Leave out payroll detail rows if your export lists them by name.
Information you give it

The budget-vs-actuals export from your accounting system, as a CSV or PDF, plus last quarter's board narrative if you have one. That narrative is the voice to match.

The recipe
  1. Export budget-vs-actuals
    Pull the standard budget-vs-actuals report from your accounting system, as a CSV or PDF.
  2. Remove payroll-by-person rows if present
    If the export lists salary by name, delete those rows or roll them into a single "personnel" line before it goes anywhere.
  3. Attach the file and paste this prompt
    Attach the cleaned export to a new chat, then paste this. Swap in last quarter's narrative if you have one.

    You don't need to understand every word below. It's written for the AI to read, not for you. Swap in the [bracketed] parts and copy the rest exactly as it is.

    Chat prompt 
    Write a plain-language financial summary for a nonprofit board
using ONLY the numbers in the attached file. Never compute a
number that isn't derivable from it, and cite the line item for
every figure you mention.

Structure it as:
1. Overall position, in two sentences.
2. The 3-5 variances that matter most: for each, the budget
   figure, the actual figure, and the difference.
3. Anything trending toward a problem if the pattern continues.
4. Three questions a board member should ask.

If a row is ambiguous, flag it and say why, don't interpret it
for me. [Optional: match the tone of this prior board narrative:
PASTE PRIOR NARRATIVE]
    ROLE:
You are a nonprofit finance translator. You explain numbers that
already exist; you do not produce new ones.

GOAL:
A plain-language financial summary a board can read in five
minutes and ask good questions about.

CONTEXT:
Use ONLY the numbers in the attached file. Never compute a number
that isn't derivable from it. The audience is a volunteer board,
smart people, not accountants.
[Optional: match the tone of this prior board narrative: PASTE
PRIOR NARRATIVE]

STEPS:
1. Read the whole file before writing.
2. State the overall position in two sentences.
3. Identify the 3–5 variances that matter most. For each, give
   the budget figure, the actual figure, and the difference.
4. Name anything trending toward a problem if the pattern
   continues.
5. Cite the line item for every figure you mention.
6. If a row is ambiguous, flag it and say why, don't interpret
   it for me.

OUTPUT:
Four short sections in that order, then three questions a board
member should ask, then the list of ambiguous rows you flagged.
  4. Check every number against the export
    The citations make this a 10-minute job rather than an afternoon. Line up each cited figure against the source file.
  5. Rewrite the judgment calls in your own words
    The AI can do the arithmetic but it doesn't know your context. "The variance is a timing issue with the state contract" is something only you know, so add it yourself.
  6. ED reviews before the packet goes out
    A final read from the person accountable for the numbers, before the summary joins the board packet.
What you walk away with

A cited, plain-language draft of the financial narrative section, ready for your own judgment calls and the ED's sign-off. The writing and rewriting that used to take an afternoon is already done.

The numbers are yours to verify, and the interpretation is yours to own. This recipe translates the figures. It doesn't analyze strategy. If you can't personally defend a variance explanation to the board, it doesn't go in the summary.
